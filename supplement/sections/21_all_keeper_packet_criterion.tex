\subsection{An all-keeper packet criterion}
\label{sec:all-keeper-packet-criterion}

Let $D$ be a finite nonempty strongly connected all-deficient oriented
graph minimal under deletion of arbitrary nonempty arc sets. All
failure, convenience, protection, and reachability predicates refer
to $D$. Fix $u$ and suppose
\[
 \mathcal M(u)\cap P(u)=\varnothing.
 \tag{AK1}\label{eq:all-keeper-no-missing-far-in}
\]
Choose one canonical reference placing all good missing partners of
$u$ before $u$. This is possible because original missing pairs are
incomparable in the protection partial order.

\begin{lemma}[All-keeper packet completion]
\label{lem:all-keeper-packet-completion}
If every chosen bad direction has all its failure sources outside
$P(u)\cup\{u\}$, then $u$ is strictly deficient in the completed
tournament $T$. No bound on $\eta(u)$ or $t(u)$ is needed.

If $P(u)=\varnothing$, its neighborhoods are unchanged. Otherwise,
with the usual packet notation $B_u,k,s,E,L$, one has exactly
\[
 J=N_T^+(u)\setminus N_D^+(u)=\mathcal M(u)\setminus B_u
 \tag{AK2}
\]
and the simultaneous envelope
\[
 N_T^{++}(u)\subseteq (N_D^{++}(u)\cup L)\setminus J,
 \qquad J\subseteq N_D^{++}(u)\cup L.
 \tag{AK3}
\]
Consequently
\[
 g_T(u)\ge
 \begin{cases}|P(u)|+1,&s=0,\\|P(u)|+g_D(u)+1,&s>0.\end{cases}
 \tag{AK4}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If a bad incident pair $ur$ were completed by $r\to u$, its
nonempty failure set would contain some $q$ with $q\to r$ and
$u\in U_D(q)$. Thus $q\in P(u)$, forbidden by the direction
selection. Every incident bad pair is therefore directed $u\to r$.
Its opposite-direction witness $q$ satisfies $q\in P(u)$ and
$q\to r$. If $P(u)$ is empty there are no bad incident pairs.
Every incoming good direction into $u$ is then convenient and is
selected by the reference, so $J=\varnothing$. No chosen direction
fails at $u$, hence its exact second neighborhood is unchanged.

Assume $P=P(u)\ne\varnothing$. Write
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 Q=N_D^{--}(u),\quad C=\partial P,\quad X=\partial^2P,\quad H=Q\setminus C,\\
 B_u=H\cap\mathcal M(u),\quad k=|B_u|,\quad s=|H\cap N_D^+(u)|.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The partner packet gives
\[
 E=(\mathcal M(u)\setminus B_u)\,\dot\cup\,X,
 \quad L=E\setminus N_{\widehat D}^{++}(u),
 \quad |L|\le
 \begin{cases}\eta(u)-2k,&s=0,\\\eta(u)-g_D(u)-2k,&s>0.\end{cases}
\]
All nonexceptional missing partners lie in $P\cup C$, and (AK1)
puts them in $C$. Every such good partner $r$ has a predecessor
$q\in P$ pointing to it, so $r\to u$ is nonconvenient and the
chosen good direction is $u\to r$. Every good exceptional partner
admits the convenient incoming direction, selected by the reference.
A bad exceptional partner is impossible: every incident bad pair
has the original predecessor $q\in P$ identified above; (AK1)
then makes its missing endpoint a member of $C$, not $H$.
This proves (AK2). Each $r\in J$ has an original predecessor
$q_r\in P$ pointing to $r$, whether its chosen first arc is good
or bad.

Through an original first intermediate of $u$, an originally far
second head would require a chosen direction failing at $u$,
which is excluded. Through an added first intermediate $r\in J$,
original far second heads lie in $L$ by the partner-head budget.
Suppose an originally far head $y\notin L$ receives an added
direction $r\to y$. Every vertex reachable from $P$ within two
steps is in $P\cup C\cup X$, and every vertex of this union far
from $u$ belongs to $L$. Thus $y\in U_D(q_r)$. The direction
$r\to y$ fails at $q_r\in P$. It cannot be a chosen good
direction, because it is nonconvenient; and it cannot be a chosen
bad direction, because such failures were excluded. The first
inclusion in (AK3) follows, with $J$ removed since distance two
is exact. The second follows because $J\subseteq E$, and a
missing endpoint reached in $\widehat D$ was already an original
exact second neighbor.

Now $h=|J|=\mu(u)-k$, so the envelope gives
$g_T(u)\ge g_D(u)+2h-|L|$. Substituting the packet bound and
the identity $|P(u)|=2\mu(u)+g_D(u)-1-\eta(u)$ gives (AK4).
No comparison between different completions is involved.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A sufficient condition for the bad directions]
\label{cor:all-keeper-independent-far-in}
If (AK1) holds and the cross-witness graph induced by $P(u)$ has
no edge, then bad pairs can all be directed as required by the lemma.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every cross-witness edge is an original missing pair. By (AK1)
there is no cross-witness edge from $u$ to $P(u)$; by hypothesis
there is none inside $P(u)$. Thus $P(u)\cup\{u\}$ is independent
in the cross-witness graph. If both directional failure sets of a
bad pair intersected this set, their opposite sources would form
an edge inside it. Therefore at least one direction avoids the
whole set. Select that direction independently for each bad pair.
\end{proof}
