\section{Fixing only the directions actually forced by protection}
\label{sec:protection-forced-completion}

For an oriented graph $D$, write $a\triangleleft w$ when
$a\to w$ and $N_D^-(a)\subseteq N_D^-(w)\setminus\{a\}$.
As in the preceding draft this is a strict partial order, and $a$ is
an originally protected far head of $w$. A direction on a missing pair
is called \emph{protection-forced} if
\begin{equation}
 a\to b\text{ is forced}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists w:\ a\triangleleft w,\quad w\to b\in A(D),\quad ab
 \text{ is missing}.
 \label{eq:protection-forced-definition}
\end{equation}
Here ``forced'' is relative to preserving all original protection
relations, not to preserving every original convenient direction.

\begin{lemma}[Forced directions are consistent and convenient]
\label{lem:protection-forced-consistency}
Each forced direction is uniquely convenient. Every tournament
completion which preserves all original protected pairs must orient
that missing pair in its forced direction. In particular two opposite
directions on the same pair cannot both be forced.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $a\triangleleft w$ and $w\to b$ witness $a\to b$.
Each original predecessor $p$ of $a$ also points to $w$, hence reaches
$b$ via $p\to w\to b$. Thus $a\to b$ is convenient.
Conversely $w$ is an original predecessor of $b$, but cannot reach
its protected far head $a$ within two steps; it witnesses failure of
$b\to a$. The direction is uniquely convenient.
In a completed tournament, preservation of $a\triangleleft w$
forces every outneighbor of $w$ to be an outneighbor of $a$, so
$a\to b$ is mandatory. Opposite forced directions would both be
convenient while each has a witnessed nonconvenient reverse, which
is impossible.
\end{proof}

Fix these directions, but release \emph{all other} missing pairs:
this includes bad pairs, pairs convenient in both directions, and
uniquely convenient pairs not forced by protection. For any linear
extension $\prec$ of original protection, orient every released pair
forward in $\prec$. Call the resulting tournament $T_\prec$.
This fixes fewer convenient directions than the preceding canonical
family, but is an alternative reference family, not a guaranteed
set-theoretic superset: the remaining released directions must now
all arise from the same order. Its median-score optimization nevertheless
dominates the old canonical-family optimization, as proved below.

\begin{theorem}[The fewer-fixed-arcs family preserves original protection]
\label{thm:protection-forced-completion-preserves}
Every $T_\prec$ preserves every original relation $y\triangleleft x$.
Consequently, with $K_x=N_{T_\prec}^+(x)\setminus N_D^+(x)$,
\[
 N_{T_\prec}^{++}(x)\subseteq
 (N_D^{++}(x)\setminus K_x)\cup
 (\mathcal F_D(x)\setminus K_x),
\]
and the exact identity $g_{T_\prec}(x)-g_D(x)=2|K_x|-c_x$ holds
with $c_x\le t_D(x)$ as before. There is no residual or support bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $y\triangleleft x$. Only a newly incoming arc $q\to y$ needs
checking for the required incoming-neighborhood nesting.

If $q\to y$ is forced, choose $w$ with $q\triangleleft w$ and
$w\to y$ original. Since $y\triangleleft x$, $w\to x$ is original.
If $qx$ is missing it is forced $q\to x$, using the same $w$.
If $x\to q$ were original, $q\triangleleft w$ would imply
$x\to w$, contrary to $w\to x$. Thus an original $qx$ also has
the required direction $q\to x$.

Otherwise $qy$ is released, so $q\prec y\prec x$. An original
$x\to q$ would make $y\to q$ forced, using $y\triangleleft x$;
that contradicts release of $qy$. Similarly, a missing pair $qx$
forced $x\to q$ has a witness $w$ with $x\triangleleft w$ and
$w\to q$ original. Transitivity gives $y\triangleleft w$, again
forcing $y\to q$, a contradiction. In all remaining cases $q\to x$
is original, forced, or released forward. Thus every predecessor of
$y$ also points to $x$ in $T_\prec$.

This proves preservation. A protected far head remains far: if
$x\to z\to y$ existed in the completed tournament, $z\to y$
would force $z\to x$, a digon. Such a head cannot be newly first
either, because $y\to x$ was original. Every original exact second
path survives unless its endpoint becomes first; the claimed envelope
and the disjoint-cost identity follow directly.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why simply releasing every direction is false.}
The three-vertex original graph $1\to0\to2$ has protection
$1\triangleleft0$. The protection extension $2\prec1\prec0$
would orient the missing pair as $2\to1$ if every pair were released,
creating $0\to2\to1$ and destroying protection. Here $1\to2$ is
exactly a forced direction under the definition. The theorem does
\emph{not} use the false statement that original incoming protection
already supplies full outgoing domination in an incomplete graph.

\section{A maximal fewer-fixed-arcs completion has no added feed out-arcs}
\label{sec:protection-forced-maximal-feed}

\begin{theorem}[All released arcs are forward in every median order]
\label{thm:protection-forced-maximal-rigidity}
There is a fewer-fixed-arcs completion $T_*$ for which every global
median order extends original protection and puts every released
added arc forward. Every median feed $f$ of this fixed $T_*$ satisfies
\[
 K_f=\varnothing,\qquad N_{T_*}^+(f)=N_D^+(f).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In a tournament an originally protected pair now has both incoming
and outgoing domination. If $y\triangleleft x$, the usual two-position
swap shows that no median order can put $x$ before $y$;
the mutual arc is gained and no intermediate contribution is lost.
Hence every median order of a fewer-fixed-arcs completion extends original
protection.

Maximize the forward score over all pairs consisting of a fewer-fixed-arcs
completion and an order, and choose $T_*$ attaining it. If any released
added arc were backward in one of its median orders $L$, re-complete
using $L$ as reference. It is admissible by protection preservation.
Forced arcs stay fixed, and precisely the backward released arcs
become forward, strictly increasing the score. This is impossible.
The argument applies to every median order of the same maximizing
completion because all have the same median score.

A feed has no outgoing released arc, since every such arc would be
backward. Nor can it be the tail $a$ of a forced arc: its witness
$a\triangleleft w$ puts $a$ before $w$ in every median order.
Thus it has no outgoing added arc of either type.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Optimization over all protection-preserving completions]
\label{lem:protection-forced-all-completion-normalization}
The maximum median score over the fewer-fixed-arcs reference family
equals the maximum over \emph{all} tournament completions preserving
original protection. In particular it is at least the maximum score
over the preceding canonical family. No corresponding dominance of
the \emph{weighted correction-budget} objective is asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take any protection-preserving completion $T$ and one of its median
orders $L$. The order extends original protection. Every forced pair
has the same direction in $T$ by its mandatory-direction lemma.
Normalize every remaining missing pair forward in $L$, obtaining a
valid fewer-fixed-arcs completion. The order's forward score increases
by exactly the number of previously backward released arcs, and none
of its original or forced arcs changes. Thus its median score is at
least that of $T$. The reverse inequality follows because the reference
family consists of protection-preserving completions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A single fixed partial graph controls the optimization]
\label{lem:protection-forced-fixed-partial-median}
Let $Q$ be obtained from $D$ by adding precisely its protection-forced
directions, and let $r$ be the number of released missing pairs. Then
\[
 \max_{\prec}\operatorname{medscore}(T_\prec)
       =\operatorname{medscore}(Q)+r.
\]
Every median order of a maximizing $T_*$ is a median order of $Q$.
Conversely every median order of $Q$, completed by orienting all
released pairs forward in that order, attains the maximum above.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In $Q$ each original relation $y\triangleleft x$ already has both
incoming and outgoing neighborhood nesting. Incoming nesting follows
from the forced-arc case in the previous preservation proof. For
outgoing nesting, an original $x\to q$ makes $y\to q$ original or
forced. A forced $x\to q$ has a witness $x\triangleleft w$ and
$w\to q$ original; then $y\triangleleft w$ forces $y\to q$ if
missing. An opposite original $q\to y$ would give $q\to x$ original,
contrary to missing $xq$.

The two-position swap for two-sided domination does not require a
complete graph: absent arcs count zero and nesting ensures no lost
intermediate contribution. Thus every median order of $Q$ extends
original protection. Completing such an order yields its maximum
$Q$ score plus all $r$ released arcs. Conversely every order in any
reference completion has score at most this number, since its $Q$
part scores at most $\operatorname{medscore}(Q)$ and its released
part at most $r$. Equality forces both component maxima.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Both original neighborhoods survive at a partial median feed]
\label{cor:protection-forced-partial-feed-exact}
Every median feed $f$ of the fixed partial graph $Q$ satisfies
\[
 N_Q^+(f)=N_D^+(f),\qquad N_Q^{++}(f)=N_D^{++}(f).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every median order of $Q$ respects original protection. A tail of a
forced arc protects an original vertex and so cannot be a feed.
Thus $f$ has no added first arc. Any new two-step path at $f$ would
use an original first arc and a forced second arc, but that second
direction is convenient, so its original predecessor $f$ already
reaches the head within two steps in $D$. The exact first set is
unchanged, which proves equality of the exact second sets as well.
\end{proof}

This identifies the remaining task with obtaining an appropriate
Seymour median feed in this fixed \emph{incomplete} graph $Q$ under
the extremal hypotheses. The tournament feed theorem must not be
applied directly to $Q$.

\begin{corollary}[An unbounded exact feed obstruction]
\label{cor:protection-forced-feed-cost}
If $D$ were all-deficient, every median feed $f$ of this $T_*$ would
have at least $g_D(f)$ newly reached unprotected far heads, with no
added first neighbors. Each new head is reached via an original
first arc followed by a released, nonconvenient added arc. None of
these second arcs can be protection-forced.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The feed theorem gives $g_{T_*}(f)\le0$. The exact first set and
survival of all original second paths give
$g_{T_*}(f)=g_D(f)-|A_f|$, so $|A_f|\ge g_D(f)$.
There is no added first intermediate. Every new-head second arc
fails convenience at $f$, whereas every forced arc is convenient.
Protection preservation excludes every originally protected far head.
\end{proof}

This is stronger first-neighborhood control than the preceding
canonical family, where a feed could still have an outgoing uniquely
convenient keeper. It is not a full SNC proof: a released uniquely
convenient pair may now be chosen \emph{against} its convenient
direction, so the feed may acquire far heads even without witnessing
a genuinely bad missing pair. The old actual-bad-source conclusions
and direct good-completion transfer cannot be carried over unchanged.

\paragraph{An exact control against automatic feed transfer.}
Take the directed four-cycle blow-up with independent class sizes
$(3,2,4,1)$ and full arcs $C_a\to C_{a+1}$ cyclically. There are
no original protection relations, so $Q=D$. Its out masks, with
the classes consecutive in the displayed order, are
\[
 (24,24,24,480,480,512,512,512,512,7).
\]
The graph has $21$ arcs and $24$ directed four-cycles. Each arc lies
in at most eight of those cycles; exactly the three arcs from the
last class to the first attain eight. Every feedback arc set has
size at least three. Equality forces all three selected arcs to be
these last-to-first arcs. Deleting them makes the graph acyclic.
Thus every global median order places the four classes consecutively
in their cyclic order. Its forward score is $18$, and there are
$3!2!4!=288$ such orders. Every possible maximizing-family feed is
the singleton last-class vertex $f=9$.

Here $d_D^+(f)=3$ but $d_D^{++}(f)=2$. Completing all released
pairs forward creates four extra second heads in the third class;
$K_f=\varnothing$ as proved, but $g_T(f)=-3$. There are Seymour
vertices elsewhere, so this is not an SNC counterexample. It is the
unweighted clone realization of the weighted four-cycle warning at
the end of Section~2 of Havet--Thomasse's median-order paper.
Its formal residual is $\eta(f)=1$ but $t(f)=4$, so it violates the
\emph{minimal-class} unit budget. The new framework must
therefore still use the extremal counterexample hypotheses rather
than treating no added first neighbors as a complete transfer proof.

\paragraph{A unit-feed control that violates the full-boundary hypothesis.}
Another graph has out masks
\[
 (30,224,96,96,2,320,1,1,1).
\]
Name its vertices $(f,a,b,c,d,r,w,z,y)$ in that order. Its original
arcs are $f\to a,b,c,d$, $a\to r,w,z$, $b,c\to r,w$, $d\to a$,
$r\to w,y$, and $w,z,y\to f$. The original protected pairs include
$d\triangleleft a$ and $r\triangleleft w$. The forced additions are
$d\to r,w,z$ and $r\to f$, so $Q$ has $21$ arcs.

Four arc-disjoint $Q$ cycles are $f a z f$, $f b w f$, $f c r f$,
and $f d a r y f$. Thus every median order has at least four backward
arcs. The order $(d,a,b,c,r,w,z,y,f)$ has exactly four, so it is
globally median with forward score $17$. Completing its released
pairs forward gives $K_f=\varnothing$ and introduces just the head
$y$, changing its gap from one to zero. Numerically
$\mu(f)=1$, $P(f)=\{d\}$, $\eta(f)=1$, and $t(f)=1$.
The missing opposite witness $r\to f$ is protection-forced, so
sedimentation does not contradict released-arc rigidity by reversing
that arc.

This graph is \emph{not} a minimal all-deficient graph: it has many
Seymour vertices. More specifically its external packet has
$\partial P(f)=\{a\}$ but $N_D^{--}(f)=\{a,b,c,r\}$. Therefore
the minimal-class full-boundary lemma at an active unit source fails.
In bundle notation $j=3$, $s=2$, and the required minimal inequality
$1\ge g(f)+2(j-s)=3$ is violated. The control does not obstruct a
counterexample-specific proof which uses that full-boundary lemma
to rule out forced incoming missing witnesses.

\paragraph{Relation to the canonical-only budget obstruction.}
For the six-vertex graph $(0,49,51,6,9,8)$ from the companion file,
there are no forced missing directions. The protection extension with
ranks $(5,2,1,0,3,4)$ gives completion $(0,49,51,7,41,9)$.
Vertex $0$ remains a sink, so $\lambda=e_0$ is an expansion density
and its correction budget is zero. This defeats the previously proved
$-1/3$ obstruction by admitting an alternative reference direction, not by changing
or disputing the canonical-only theorem. In its cloned and amplified
variants, this same orientation pattern can likewise be used internally
and mixed equally across copies to obtain zero budget.

\paragraph{Independent finite audit.}
\texttt{audit\_protection\_forced\_completion.js} tested all $59049$
five-vertex oriented graphs and $20000$ fixed-seed samples of orders
six through twelve. It checked $158098$ fewer-fixed-arcs completions,
$529572$ protected pairs, and $950484$ vertex envelopes and exact
cost identities. It encountered $195408$ genuinely released reversals
of uniquely convenient directions, so the expansion was exercised
nonvacuously. It additionally checked every median order of every
score-maximizing reference completion on $300$ sampled six-vertex
graphs, checking $11529$ median feeds with no added out-arcs.
The ten-vertex clone control's $288$ median orders and unique feed
were independently counted by exact subset dynamic programming.
These audits verify identities and attack the proofs; they do
not prove the remaining all-deficient completion-budget existence.

\paragraph{Next global targets.}
The exact weighted threshold may now be sought over this alternative
family, with only protection-forced arcs fixed. Alternatively, the
fixed maximal completion has eliminated all added first neighbors
at every median feed, isolating the obstruction to released reverse
convenient directions or bad directions which create multiple far
heads. A subsequent argument must control those heads without
assuming a globally compatible packet selection or claiming an
arc-weighted feed theorem that the previous controls refute.
