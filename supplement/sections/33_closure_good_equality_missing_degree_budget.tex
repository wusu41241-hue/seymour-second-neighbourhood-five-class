\section{Remaining missing degree is charged to original head surplus}
\label{sec:closure-good-equality-missing-degree}

Let $Q$ be the terminal iterated protection closure of an arbitrary
finite nonempty oriented graph $D$, and let $T_*$ be a maximum-score
reference completion of $Q$. Every remaining missing pair of $Q$ is
free, and every median of $T_*$ puts all free arcs forward.
Let $f$ be a median feed with $g=g_D(f)>0$,
$t=t_D(f)$ and $\mu_Q=|\mathcal M_Q(f)|$. Its first set is original,
and its completed second set is
\[
 N_{T_*}^{++}(f)=N_D^{++}(f)\,\dot\cup\,A_f,
 \qquad A_f\subseteq\mathcal F_D(f).
\]
Put $c=|A_f|$ and let $G_L$ be the good vertices of a median order
$L$ ending at $f$.

\begin{lemma}[Every newly reached original-far head is good]
\label{lem:closure-new-head-good}
One has $A_f\subseteq G_L$ in every median order ending at $f$.
Writing
$b_L=|N_D^{++}(f)\setminus G_L|$ and
$s_L=|G_L|-|N_{T_*}^+(f)|$, the exact identity is
\[
 s_L=c-g-b_L\ge0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A new head $y$ has a path $f\to a\to y$ with original first arc.
The second arc cannot belong to $Q$, whose exact second neighborhood
at the feed is still original. Thus it is a free completion direction,
which is forward in every median order. Its first intermediate $a$
occurs before $y$, so $y$ is good. Consequently the good set consists
of all $c$ new heads and $d_D^{++}(f)-b_L$ old heads. Subtract the
original first count $d_D^{++}(f)+g$ to obtain the identity. The weak
inequality is the strong feed theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The stronger equality budget]
\label{prop:closure-strong-feed-equality-degree-budget}
If $|G_L|=|N_{T_*}^+(f)|$, then
\[
 \mu_Q\le t-g.
\]
This includes negative completed gap as well as zero completed gap.
No arc-minimality, source-support or residual assumption is needed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $r\in\mathcal M_Q(f)\cap G_L$, its completed direction is the
free incoming arc $r\to f$, because the feed has no added first arc.
Equality permits global sedimentation. It places $f$ before $r$
while preserving the median score, making this free arc backward in
another median of the same $T_*$. All-median rigidity excludes this.
Therefore
\[
 |\mathcal M_Q(f)\cap N_{T_*}^{++}(f)|
 \le |N_{T_*}^{++}(f)\setminus G_L|
=d_D^{++}(f)+c-d_D^+(f)=c-g.
\]
In fact every such remaining partner in the second set is an original
\emph{nongood} second neighbor: the new-head lemma excludes all of
$A_f$ from this nongood class.
Every remaining missing partner not in the completed second set is
far in $T_*$, since it is not a completed first neighbor either.
It was originally missing, hence cannot be originally protected,
and belongs to $\mathcal F_D(f)\setminus A_f$. Consequently
\[
 |\mathcal M_Q(f)\cap U_{T_*}(f)|\le t-c.
\]
The two classes partition $\mathcal M_Q(f)$. Their sum is at most
$(c-g)+(t-c)=t-g$, proving the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Large remaining degree forces stable medians]
\label{cor:closure-large-degree-strong-good-slack}
If $\mu_Q>t-g$, every median order ending at $f$ has strict strong-feed
slack $|G_L|>|N_{T_*}^+(f)|$. In particular it is stable under the
Havet--Thomasse sedimentation operation.
If $t=2$, the strict original-surplus theorem gives $g=1$;
strong-feed equality then forces $\mu_Q=1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The strong feed theorem always gives the weak inequality. Equality
contradicts the proposition, so all such orders have strict inequality.
For $t=2$, positive original gap and $t\ge g+1$ give $g=1$.
The proposition gives $\mu_Q\le1$, while terminal whole-feed exclusion
gives $\mu_Q\ge1$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Remaining scope.}
The budget does not apply automatically to medians with strict good
slack. It does not assert that every median order has equality, nor
that every remaining missing partner is far when the completed gap is
negative. It separates the two possible global obstructions rather
than resolving either one in arbitrary size.
