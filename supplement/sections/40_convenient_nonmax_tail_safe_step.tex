\section{A convenient step from an already nonmaximal protected tail}
\label{sec:convenient-nonmax-tail-step}

All predicates in this section refer to the \emph{current} graph $H$.
Recall that $a\triangleleft_H w$ means $a\to w$ and
$N_H^-(a)\subseteq N_H^-(w)\setminus\{a\}$.

\begin{lemma}[A larger safe single-step operator]
\label{lem:convenient-nonmax-tail-safe-step}
Suppose $ab$ is missing, $b$ has no current protection successor,
$a$ has some current protection successor $w$, and the direction
$a\to b$ is convenient in $H$. Adding only $a\to b$ preserves every
current protection. Its tail still has a protection successor, and
the new arc is convenient relative to the graph before its addition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Only the incoming neighborhood of $b$ changes. A protected relation
whose protected head is $b$ cannot exist, by its maximality. A relation
whose target is $b$ becomes easier to satisfy because the target's
incoming set increases. Every other incoming-neighborhood comparison
is unchanged. Thus every old protection survives, including
$a\triangleleft_H w$. Convenience before addition is an explicit
assumption, not inferred from protection or from the original graph.
The graph remains oriented because the pair was missing.
\end{proof}

One may add these steps to the previously FORCED/WEAK closure,
performing them singly and recomputing protection after each step.
At most the original missing-pair count many nonterminal additions
are possible. Every added arc has a tail with a protection successor
which survives to termination. If all FORCED operations are also
exhausted, terminal protection has both incoming and outgoing nesting.

\begin{corollary}[The exact original feed invariants survive]
\label{cor:convenient-nonmax-tail-feed-invariants}
Complete the terminal partial graph $Q$ by all remaining directions
forward in a protection extension and maximize global median score
over \emph{all} such extensions. At every feed $f$,
\[
 N_T^+(f)=N_Q^+(f)=N_D^+(f),\qquad
 N_Q^{++}(f)=N_D^{++}(f).
\]
Every original protected far target stays protected and far. Every new
original-far second head in $T$ is good in every median order ending
at $f$. In particular the previously strict-original-surplus
and strong-equality missing-degree proofs continue to apply.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Original protections survive every safe step and the final completion.
Every added arc's tail has a surviving protection successor and cannot
be last in a median order. The feed consequently has no added first
arc at any stage. A new second direction at that feed uses an original
first step and an arc convenient in its preceding graph, so it cannot
create an exact second head not previously present. Induction gives
the exact original second set in $Q$. At the final maximizing
completion all remaining free arcs are forward in every median,
by the all-reference closure argument. A newly reached original-far
head therefore has a forward free second arc from an original first
neighbor, making it good. The earlier surplus and equality-degree
arguments use exactly these invariants and free-arc rigidity.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Terminal obstruction.}
At a terminal feed $f$, if $r$ is still missing from $f$ and has a
current protection successor, the reverse direction $r\to f$ must
be \emph{currently} nonconvenient; otherwise the new safe operator
could add it. Original convenience is insufficient for this claim,
because closure may have added new predecessors of $r$.

\paragraph{Important scope limit.}
The FORCED/WEAK original-partner cycle and seed-path lifting proofs
do not automatically apply to this enlarged operator. A convenient
direction need not shortcut an old two-step path \emph{inside} the
fixed partner set, and no such shortcut is asserted here. In particular
the residual-four zero-gap equality shape which used an original
partner triangle cannot be transferred without a separate lifting
proof. The operator preserves the stated feed invariants; it has not
been shown to settle any remaining multiple-heavy counterexample class.
