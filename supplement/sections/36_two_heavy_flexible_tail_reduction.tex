\section{A flexible pair between two uncontrolled actual sources}
\label{sec:two-heavy-flexible-tail-reduction}

All predicates refer to the original finite nonempty oriented graph
$D$. Let $A$ be its actual bad-direction failure sources. Suppose that
$A\setminus\{u,v\}$ has unprotected far-head budget at most one,
and every cross-witness pair between two sources of this set is
flexible (bad or double-convenient good). No budget restriction is
made on $u,v$ or inactive vertices. Assume $uv$ itself is a flexible
original missing pair.

For every linear extension $\sigma$ of original protection, complete
every flexible pair forward in $\sigma$ and fix uniquely convenient
good directions. Maximize the unweighted forward score over this
\emph{complete family of all protection extensions} and all orders.
Every median order of a maximizing member extends original protection
and puts every flexible added direction forward. Indeed, any backward
flexible pair could be made forward by re-referencing to that median
order, while leaving original and fixed directions unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[The head of the two-exception flexible pair is a feed]
\label{thm:two-heavy-flexible-head-feed}
Suppose $D$ is all-deficient. In any score-maximizing reference
completion $T$ in which $uv$ is directed $u\to v$, a global median
order maximizing $\operatorname{pos}(v)$ among median orders of
this fixed $T$ has feed $v$. Consequently $v$ has no successor in
original protection. The theorem applies in arbitrary finite size.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $L$ be the indicated median order and $f$ its feed. A feed outside
$A$ is impossible: reverse all its added out-arcs inward, preserving
global optimality. Its first neighborhood is original, and any new
far second target would require a bad directional failure at $f$ or
a nonconvenient chosen good direction. Both are impossible, so feed
SNP would transfer to the originally deficient vertex.

If $f=u$, the flexible direction $u\to v$ would be backward in $L$,
contradicting maximum-reference rigidity. Suppose that
$f\in A\setminus\{u,v\}$. The universal simultaneous envelope
and its $t(f)\le1$ force
\[
 K_f=\varnothing,\qquad g_D(f)=t(f)=1,\qquad g_T(f)=0,
 \qquad G_L=N_T^{++}(f)=N_D^{++}(f)\cup\{y\},
\]
where $y$ is its sole unprotected original far head. Its new head
uses an original first arc and an added bad second direction.
Choose an opposite witness $r$. The original missing pair $fr$ has
$r\ne y$, so $r\in N_D^{++}(f)\subseteq G_L$.

If $r\notin\{u,v\}$, its cross-witness pair $fr$ is flexible by
hypothesis and has completed direction $r\to f$ because $K_f$ is
empty. Sedimentation makes this direction backward in another global
median order of $T$, contradicting maximum-reference rigidity.
If $r=v$, sedimentation strictly increases the position of $v$,
contradicting the selected median order.

It remains that $r=u$. If $v$ is not an original protected far target
of $f$, it is an original first target, an original exact second
target, or the sole unprotected original far head $y$. Thus
$v\in N_T^+(f)\cup G_L$, and sedimentation increases its position,
again contradicting the selected order. If instead
$v\triangleleft f$, then $v$ remains far from $f$ in $T$ and is a
bad vertex of $L$. Sedimentation places $v$ before the feed $f$ and
$u\in G_L$ after it. It therefore puts $v$ before $u$, making the
fixed tournament's flexible arc $u\to v$ backward in another median
order. This too contradicts maximum-reference rigidity.

All cases except $f=v$ are impossible. Every median order extends
original protection, so its last position at $v$ forbids every
original relation $v\triangleleft q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two nonmaximal exceptions with a flexible pair]
\label{cor:two-heavy-both-protection-successors}
Under the same budget and cross-witness-flexibility hypotheses,
if both $u$ and $v$ have successors in original protection and $uv$
is flexible, $D$ has a Seymour vertex.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In any maximum-reference completion the flexible pair has a head.
The theorem forces that head to have no original protection successor,
contradicting either of the two asserted relations.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two-heavy minimal counterexample reduction]
\label{cor:two-heavy-minimal-flexible-reduction}
In the strong arbitrary-arc-set-deletion-minimal all-deficient class,
suppose there are exactly two nonunit actual sources $u,v$, all other
actual sources having residual one. If $uv$ is bad or double-convenient
good, at least one of $u,v$ is maximal in the original protection
order. More precisely, the head of $uv$ in any maximum-reference
completion is maximal and can be chosen as a median feed of that
completion. Inactive residuals are unrestricted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Unit budgets and the unit--unit cross-witness-tail badness
give the two hypotheses on other actual sources. Apply the theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The good-tail obstruction forced by a protected unit shadow}

\begin{lemma}[A protected unit shadow with a separate incoming path]
\label{lem:protected-unit-shadow-good-tail}
Suppose $f$ is an actual unit source in the minimal class, $uv$ and
$vf$ are original missing pairs, $v$ is an opposite witness to $f$
for some bad direction, $v$ reaches $f$ at exact distance two,
and $u\triangleleft f$. Then $v\to u$
is nonconvenient. In particular, if $uv$ is good, its only convenient
direction is $u\to v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The independently assumed incoming path and the full unit packet give
$v\in N_D^{--}(f)=\partial P(f)$. Hence some $p\in P(f)$ points
originally to $v$. Protection $u\triangleleft f$ implies that any
vertex reaching $u$ within two steps reaches $f$ within two steps:
an original predecessor of $u$ also points to $f$, and an incoming
two-step path into $u$ can replace its last arc by one into $f$.
Thus $p\in P(u)$. The original arc $p\to v$ witnesses nonconvenience
of $v\to u$. Goodness forces the other direction to be convenient.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Rejected attempted strengthening.}
The opposite-witness property alone gives $f\to v$ at exact distance
two, and does
\emph{not} imply $v\in N_D^{--}(f)$. A protected unit shadow alone
does not establish the reverse convenience failure without a separate
original path $v\leadsto f$, which is an explicit extra hypothesis of
the last lemma. No such reachability is assumed in the main theorem.

\paragraph{Remaining gap.}
This does not exclude a two-heavy minimal counterexample with a maximal
heavy source. The controlling completion may give its heavy feed more
than one newly reached far head, so unit-feed sedimentation equality
need not hold. Uniquely convenient heavy tails are outside the flexible
pair theorem. No claim that every allocation $(m_1,m_2,1^k)$ is excluded
is made.
