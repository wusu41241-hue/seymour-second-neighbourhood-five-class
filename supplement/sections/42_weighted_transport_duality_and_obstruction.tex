\section{The tournament expansion polytope has no extra density freedom}
\label{sec:weighted-transport-density-collapse}

For a tournament $T$, write $A_T$ for its adjacency matrix,
$B_T=A_T-A_T^T$, and $F_T$ for the matrix of original far relations:
$(F_T)_{xy}=1$ precisely when $x\ne y$ and $x$ does not reach $y$
within two steps. Let $N_T$ have entry $+1$ on exact second targets,
$-1$ on first targets, and zero elsewhere. Define
\[
 \mathcal P(T)=\{\lambda\ge0:\mathbf1^T\lambda=1,
                         \ N_T^T\lambda\ge0\},\qquad
 \mathcal L(T)=\{\lambda\ge0:\mathbf1^T\lambda=1,
                         \ B_T\lambda\ge0\}.
\]
The second set is exactly the set of Fisher's \emph{losing densities}.
The first set should initially be called the reverse expansion-density
polytope, rather than treating its defining inequality as the definition
of a skew losing density.

\begin{lemma}[Density collapse in tournaments]
\label{lem:transport-density-collapse}
For every tournament $T$, $\mathcal P(T)=\mathcal L(T)$.
In particular the expansion-density formulation does not provide an
additional choice of density beyond the usual tournament losing density.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every nonfirst vertex of a tournament is an incoming vertex; it is
either an exact second target or a far target. Consequently
\[
 N_T=A_T^T-A_T-F_T,\qquad
 N_T^T\lambda=B_T\lambda-F_T^T\lambda.
\]
Since $F_T^T\lambda\ge0$, membership in $\mathcal P(T)$ implies
$B_T\lambda\ge0$ and hence membership in $\mathcal L(T)$.
Conversely Fisher's tournament Lemma~2 states that, for a losing density,
the mass of exact second predecessors of each vertex is at least the
mass of its first predecessors. This is $N_T^T\lambda\ge0$.
\end{proof}

The standard uniqueness theorem for a tournament game's equilibrium
then implies that these two normalized sets are a singleton. Uniqueness
is not needed for the obstruction proofs below. The equality itself,
however, prevents an apparent ``optimize over a larger expansion
polytope'' escape from a bad Fisher losing density. In an arbitrary
oriented graph missing pairs contribute another matrix term, so the
first implication is no longer available by the same argument.

\begin{lemma}[Protected dominators carry no losing mass]
\label{lem:transport-protection-support}
If $y\triangleleft x$ is protected in a tournament and
$\lambda\in\mathcal L(T)$, then $\lambda_y=0$. Consequently, in
every protection-preserving completion of $D$, its losing density is
supported on maximal elements of the original protection poset.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Protection implies $y\to x$, and every outneighbor of $x$ is an
outneighbor of $y$. Thus the $y$ row of $B_T$ dominates the $x$ row
entrywise, with a difference of one in both the $x$ and $y$ columns.
If $\lambda_y>0$, skew-symmetry and $B_T\lambda\ge0$ imply
$(B_T\lambda)_y=0$: indeed all terms of
$\lambda^TB_T\lambda=0$ are nonnegative. But then
\[
 0=(B_T\lambda)_y\ge(B_T\lambda)_x+\lambda_x+\lambda_y>0,
\]
a contradiction. Original protected relations survive the admissible
completion, so any original vertex below another in that poset has
zero mass. This is a support reduction, not a conclusion that all
remaining sources have unit budget.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Primary source.}
D.~C.~Fisher~\cite{Fisher1996}, \emph{Squaring a tournament: A proof of Dean's conjecture},
Journal of Graph Theory 23 (1996), 43--48, Section~3, Lemma~2.
An author-paper scan is available at
\url{https://www.math.ru.nl/OpenGraphProblems/TimV/%5BDavid%20Fisher%5D%20Proof%20for%20tournaments.pdf}.
Fisher's Section~2 also records the earlier uniqueness theorem for
tournament winning densities, which applies to losing densities by
reversing the tournament.

\section{Exact duality and a weaker sufficient transfer threshold}
\label{sec:weighted-transport-exact-dual}

For a protection-preserving completion $T$ of $D$, put
$\Delta_x=g_T(x)-g_D(x)=2|K_x|-c_x$, where $c_x$ is the exact
new-head cost from the preceding draft. For fixed $T$ let
\[
 \mathsf B(D,T)=\max_{\lambda\in\mathcal P(T)}\lambda^T\Delta.
\]
The polytope is nonempty by Fisher's theorem and is compact. Ordinary
finite-dimensional linear-programming duality gives the exact formula
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf B(D,T)=
 \min_{w\ge0}\ \max_x\bigl(\Delta_x+(N_Tw)_x\bigr).
 \label{eq:transport-dual-formula}
\end{equation}
Indeed a dual upper bound $a$ has $w\ge0$ and
$\Delta+N_Tw\le a\mathbf1$. Multiplication by any feasible $\lambda$
yields $\lambda^T\Delta\le a$ because
$\lambda^TN_Tw\ge0$. The usual LP dual gives equality and attainment.
Thus an exact obstruction can be certified using a single nonnegative
\emph{head-weight vector}, without numerical trust in an LP solver.

There is also a useful correction to the earlier target threshold.
If $D$ is all-deficient, every integer gap is at least one. A normalized
$\lambda\in\mathcal P(T)$ would already yield a contradiction from
\begin{equation}
 \lambda^T\Delta>-1,
 \label{eq:transport-weaker-threshold}
\end{equation}
since $\lambda^Tg_T=\lambda^Tg_D+\lambda^T\Delta>0$, whereas
$\lambda^Tg_T=-\mathbf1^TN_T^T\lambda\le0$.
The old sufficient target $\lambda^T\Delta\ge0$ is strictly stronger
than necessary. In the minimal class $g_D\in\{1,2\}$, writing
$G_2=\{x:g_D(x)=2\}$ gives the still more accurate threshold
\[
 \lambda^T\bigl(\Delta+\mathbf1_{G_2}\bigr)>-1.
\]
These are sufficient contradiction criteria, not existence theorems.
No unrestricted completion guarantee is supplied by LP duality alone.

\section{An all-order obstruction to automatic reference-budget balance}
\label{sec:weighted-transport-infinite-obstruction}

Consider the six-vertex graph with out masks
\[
 (0,49,51,6,9,8).
\]
Equivalently its outgoing sets are
\[
 \varnothing,\quad\{0,4,5\},\quad\{0,1,4,5\},\quad
 \{1,2\},\quad\{0,3\},\quad\{3\}.
\]
For any integer $k\ge1$, replace vertex $a$ by an independent clone
class $C_a$ of size $k$, and replace each original arc by the complete
bipartite orientation between its two classes. Call the resulting
oriented graph $D_k$. Its pointwise gaps on $C_0,\ldots,C_5$ are
\[
 0,\quad 2k,\quad 3k,\quad-k,\quad0,\quad-k.
\]
It has Seymour vertices and is \emph{not} all-deficient.

Every within-class missing pair is convenient in both directions.
Between distinct classes the only missing pairs are $C_0C_3$,
$C_0C_5$, and $C_4C_5$. The first is uniquely conveniently oriented
$C_0\to C_3$, the second $C_5\to C_0$, and the third is convenient
in both directions. Hence there are no bad missing pairs at all.
The original protection relations include $C_4\triangleleft C_0$
and $C_2\triangleleft C_1$, with the notation interpreted pointwise.

A canonical completion $T$ obtained from any linear extension of
original protection therefore has transitive orders on each of
$C_0,C_1,C_2,C_3$ and on the entire union $C_4\cup C_5$; the two
uniquely convenient interclass directions are fixed. Conversely all
of these internal orders can be realized by a protection reference.
After clone relabeling, the only distinct choices are the
$\binom{2k}{k}$ binary interleavings of $C_4$ and $C_5$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact negative optimum for every reference completion]
\label{thm:transport-clone-obstruction}
For every $k\ge1$ and \emph{every} protection-reference canonical
completion $T$ of $D_k$,
\[
 \mathsf B(D_k,T)=-\frac{k}{3}.
\]
This statement quantifies over every feasible expansion density, not
only one arbitrarily selected density.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Give every vertex of $C_0,\ldots,C_5$ the respective head weight
\[
 w=(4,13/3,0,11/3,0,0).
\]
For $x\in C_a$, $a\le3$, write $s_a(x)$ for the number of later
vertices in its class. For $x\in C_4\cup C_5$ write $s_W(x)$ for
the number of later vertices in the transitive order on that union.
The ranges are $0\le s_a\le k-1$ and $0\le s_W\le2k-1$.
Direct first- and exact-second-neighborhood calculations give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{class of }x&\Delta_x&(N_Tw)_x\\\hline
 C_0&s_0-k&2k/3-4s_0\\
 C_1&s_1&-k/3-13s_1/3\\
 C_2&s_2&-14k/3\\
 C_3&s_3&-k/3-11s_3/3\\
 C_4&s_W&-10k/3\\
 C_5&k+s_W&-10k/3
\end{array}
\]
For example the first targets of $C_5$ are all of $C_3,C_0$ plus
the later vertices of $C_4\cup C_5$; its exact second targets are
exactly $C_1\cup C_2$. Added arcs inside the union are all forward,
so they do not create exact second targets earlier in its order.
The other five rows follow in the same way.
Each row of the table satisfies
\[
 \Delta_x+(N_Tw)_x\le-k/3.
\]
For $C_2,C_4,C_5$ the stronger upper bounds are respectively
$-11k/3-1$, $-4k/3-1$, and $-k/3-1$. This common dual vector
proves $\mathsf B(D_k,T)\le-k/3$.

For the matching lower bound, let $v_0,v_1,v_3$ be the last vertices
in $C_0,C_1,C_3$ respectively and put weight $1/3$ on each, zero
elsewhere. Their exact signed-neighborhood rows sum to zero:
\[
 N_T(v_0,\cdot)+N_T(v_1,\cdot)+N_T(v_3,\cdot)=0.
\]
Indeed their interclass first sets are respectively $C_3$,
$C_0\cup C_4\cup C_5$, and $C_1\cup C_2$, while their second
sets are $C_1\cup C_2$, $C_3$, and $C_0\cup C_4\cup C_5$.
They have no remaining within-class first vertices. Hence this density
satisfies $N_T^T\lambda=0$. Its three correction values are $-k,0,0$,
so its budget is exactly $-k/3$.
\end{proof}

In particular $k\ge4$ refutes even an unconditional existence claim
with the weaker threshold $\lambda^T\Delta>-1$. These graphs are
already covered by the good-missing-edge SNC theorem. Thus completion
budget balance is a \emph{sufficient but not necessary} certificate
for SNC on arbitrary graphs.

\section{The negative budget survives strong dense amplification}
\label{sec:weighted-transport-dense-obstruction}

Apply the cyclic three-copy amplification from the companion draft
$m\ge1$ times to $D_k$, obtaining $D_{k,m}$. Every missing pair
still lies inside a base copy and is good, so every canonical reference
completion independently chooses a completion of $D_k$ in each base
copy. All original gaps and all completion corrections stay the same
pointwise. Put the preceding $w$ in every base copy.
For every vertex the added full next-copy first contribution to
$N_Tw$ is canceled by the equally weighted full following-copy second
contribution. The same cancellation applies at every amplification
level. Thus $\Delta+N_Tw\le-(k/3)\mathbf1$ remains valid.

Conversely put equal total density on every base copy and use the
attaining three-point density inside each. Its external signed column
contributions cancel and the internal contributions are zero. Therefore
for every protection-reference completion $T$ of $D_{k,m}$,
\[
 \mathsf B(D_{k,m},T)=-k/3.
\]
These original graphs are strongly connected and have
\[
 n=6k3^m,\qquad \delta^+(D_{k,m})=\frac{n-6k}{2},\qquad
 \frac{\delta^+}{n}\longrightarrow\frac12.
\]
No fixed dense threshold below $1/2$, strong connectivity, the absence
of bad missing pairs, or optimization among maximal reference
completions alone can force a nonnegative budget or the weaker $>-1$
budget threshold on arbitrary SNC graphs. This does not refute a
theorem \emph{under the all-deficient arc-minimal hypothesis}: the
family violates all-deficiency and also the minimal-class gap bound
$g_D\le2$ for most of its vertices when $k$ is large.

\section{Median rigidity is not equilibrium-support rigidity}
\label{sec:weighted-transport-median-support-control}

One cannot combine maximal-reference median rigidity with the assertion
that every arc between positive losing-density vertices becomes backward
in some median order. The six-vertex tournament with out masks
\[
 (42,60,57,16,33,8)
\]
has losing density $(0,0,0,1,1,1)/3$: its skew residual, after
multiplication by three, is $(1,3,3,0,0,0)$. Its unique global median
order is
\[
 (1,2,4,0,5,3),
\]
with thirteen forward arcs. Thus $5\to3$ lies between two positive
density vertices and is forward in every median order. This is only
a control against that generic support shortcut, not an assertion
that a maximal completion arising from a minimal counterexample can
realize this particular configuration.

\paragraph{Independent verification boundary.}
The companion Node program
\path{verify_transport_obstruction_family.js} uses exact integer
arithmetic with the scaled head weights $(12,13,0,11,0,0)$.
It checks all $98$ canonical quotient types for $k=1,2,3,4$, all $224$
independently chosen three-copy types for $k=1,2$, nine iterated
amplifications through order $216$, and all $720$ orders of the final
median control. These checks confirm the supplied all-$k$ proof;
they are not a search for an SNC counterexample or a proof that the
counterexample-specific selection step is impossible.

\paragraph{Remaining global step after these repairs.}
The mathematically relevant remaining choice is the completion: in a
tournament the normalized Fisher density itself is fixed. A successful
transfer theorem may target the strict $>-1$ threshold (including
the $g_D=2$ mass correction), but it must use the true all-deficient
arc-minimal structure. Generic strong or asymptotically dense properties
do not supply that theorem. The explicit common dual also identifies
what must be ruled out: a nonnegative head-weight vector whose signed
neighborhood gain simultaneously offsets every admissible completion's
keeper gain by a negative constant.
