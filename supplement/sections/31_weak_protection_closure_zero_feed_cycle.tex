\section{Weak protection steps force a directed cycle at every zero-gap feed}
\label{sec:weak-protection-closure-zero-feed-cycle}

Use the preceding iterated closure definitions. In addition to adding
all currently FORCED directions, allow the following \emph{single-arc}
step in a current graph $H$:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 ab\text{ missing},\qquad N_H^-(a)\subseteq N_H^-(b),\\
 b\text{ is maximal in the current protection order};\quad\text{add }a\to b.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Maximal means there is no original-current relation $b\triangleleft_H x$.
This step is called weak protection. Recompute protection and forced
directions after each weak step: simultaneous arbitrary weak choices
are \emph{not} permitted. Continue until neither a forced direction nor
an eligible weak step remains; any deterministic tie rule may be used.
Termination requires at most $M(D)$ added pairs. Denote the terminal
graph by $Q^{\mathrm w}$.

\begin{lemma}[The single weak step is convenient and protection-monotone]
\label{lem:single-weak-protection-safe}
Every weak step preserves all current protections and creates
$a\triangleleft b$. Its new direction is convenient in the graph
\emph{before} the step.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Only the incoming neighborhood of $b$ changes. An old relation
$b\triangleleft_H x$ could be destroyed by the new predecessor, but
maximality excludes every such relation. An old relation
$y\triangleleft_H b$ can only become easier to satisfy when its right
vertex gains a predecessor; every other relation is unchanged.
After the addition,
$N^-(a)\subseteq N^-(b)\setminus\{a\}$, so the new arc protects $b$.
For convenience, every old predecessor of $a$ already points directly
to $b$ by the assumed containment. The graph remains oriented because
$ab$ was missing.
\end{proof}

At the terminal graph no forced missing direction remains, so current
protection has both incoming and outgoing nesting. Complete all its
remaining missing pairs forward in one current-protection extension,
and maximize the ordinary global median score over all such extensions.
Call a maximizing tournament $T^{\mathrm w}_*$.

The preceding closure proof applies without change: every added arc
has a tail with a protection successor which survives to the end, and
every step was convenient relative to its preceding graph. Thus, at
every global median feed $f$,
\[
 N_{T^{\mathrm w}_*}^+(f)=N_{Q^{\mathrm w}}^+(f)=N_D^+(f),\qquad
 N_{Q^{\mathrm w}}^{++}(f)=N_D^{++}(f).
\]
Every median respects terminal protection and makes all still-free
added arcs forward. No intermediate graph is assumed all-deficient,
strongly connected or minimal.

\begin{theorem}[The terminal missing partners of a zero-gap feed contain a cycle]
\label{thm:weak-closure-zero-feed-cycle}
Let $f$ be any median feed of $T^{\mathrm w}_*$ such that
$g_D(f)>0$ and $g_{T^{\mathrm w}_*}(f)=0$. Then
\[
 R=\mathcal M_{Q^{\mathrm w}}(f)\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad \delta^-(Q^{\mathrm w}[R])\ge1.
\]
In fact the \emph{original} induced graph $D[R]$ already contains a
directed cycle. In particular a cycle of length at least three exists, and
\[
 |R|\ge3,\qquad
 t_D(f)\ge g_D(f)+|R|\ge g_D(f)+3.
\]
Neither minimality nor actual BAD failure-source status is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding arbitrary-closure proof gives $R\ne\varnothing$ and
shows that every member of $R$ is far from $f$ in the completed
tournament: otherwise it belongs to the equality good set, and
global sedimentation would reverse its free incoming arc to $f$.
Also $R\subseteq\mathcal F_D(f)\setminus A_f$ and
$|A_f|=g_D(f)$, where $A_f$ is the set of new original-far second heads.
This gives $t_D(f)\ge g_D(f)+|R|$.

Fix $r\in R$. The feed $f$ is maximal in terminal protection, since
every median order extends that order. There is no eligible weak
$r\to f$ step, so
\[
 N_{Q^{\mathrm w}}^-(r)\not\subseteq N_{Q^{\mathrm w}}^-(f).
\]
Choose a witness $p$ in the difference. Since $r$ is far from $f$
in the tournament and $p\to r$ is retained, the alternative $f\to p$
would give $f\to p\to r$. Therefore $p\to f$ in the tournament.
It was not an arc of the terminal graph by choice of $p$, and the
opposite direction could not have been an arc there either. Thus
$pf$ is a remaining missing pair, so $p\in R$. Also $p\ne r$,
because the graph is loopless. Hence every $r\in R$ has a predecessor
inside $Q^{\mathrm w}[R]$. Following predecessors in a finite graph
produces a directed cycle. Loops and directed two-cycles are forbidden,
so at least three distinct vertices occur.

It remains to lift cycle existence back to $D[R]$. Hold this final set
$R$ fixed throughout the earlier construction. At a FORCED step which
adds an internal arc $a\to b$ of $R$, let $a\triangleleft_H w$ and
$w\to b$ be its preceding-graph witness. Since $b$ is far from $f$ in
the final tournament, the retained arc $w\to b$ implies $w\to f$ there.
If $wf$ were adjacent in the terminal graph, its direction would already
be $w\to f$. The retained relation $a\triangleleft w$ would then force
the still-missing $a\to f$, contradicting terminality. Thus $w\in R$.
The new internal arc is merely a shortcut of the old internal path
$a\to w\to b$. Adding all such shortcuts cannot make an acyclic
induced graph cyclic.

At a single WEAK step adding an internal $a\to b$, suppose an acyclic
previous induced graph would become cyclic. Then it previously had a
path $b\leadsto p\to a$ inside $R$. Its length cannot be one since
$ab$ was missing. The old containment $N_H^-(a)\subseteq N_H^-(b)$
gives $p\to b$ already, so the previous graph itself had the cycle
$b\leadsto p\to b$, a contradiction. Steps outside $R$ do not alter its
induced arcs. Hence an acyclic $D[R]$ would stay acyclic throughout,
contrary to the terminal cycle just proved. The original cycle exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Low-budget feeds are strictly, not equally, Seymour]
\label{cor:weak-closure-low-budget-strict-feed}
At a feed with $g_D(f)>0$, either
\[
 g_{T^{\mathrm w}_*}(f)\le-1,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 t_D(f)\ge g_D(f)+3.
\]
In particular if $t_D(f)\le g_D(f)+2$, the completed feed has strict
SNP. If $t_D(f)\le3$, a zero completed gap is impossible.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The feed theorem gives $g_{T^{\mathrm w}_*}(f)\le0$. The theorem excludes
the equality case under either displayed budget restriction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope and remaining obstruction.}
The stronger $+3$ budget is asserted only for a \emph{zero completed
gap}. If the completed gap is negative, the general strict original
surplus remains $t_D\ge g_D+1$, not $g_D+3$. A feed may have strict
SNP only because it acquired two or more original-far second heads;
its original gap can still be positive. No full SNC proof follows.
Although a cycle is now proved to exist in the original graph $D[R]$,
its vertices need not be actual BAD sources in $D$. It must not be counted
as three additional original positive-residual vertices without a
separate argument. Positive indegree is proved for the terminal induced
graph, not for every vertex of the original induced graph.
