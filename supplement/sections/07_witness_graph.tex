\subsection{Using only cross-witness pairs as the source graph}
\label{sec:witness-graph-refinement}

Let $D$ be an arbitrary finite nonempty oriented graph, with the
failure sets, protection relation, unprotected-target count $t$, and
canonical convenient orientations defined in
Section~\ref{sec:small-failure-sources}. Let $Z$ contain all directional
failure sources of every bad missing pair. No bound on $|Z|$ is imposed.
Define the undirected \emph{cross-witness graph} $\mathcal B_Z$ on $Z$
by putting an edge $xy$ whenever some bad missing pair $ab$ has
$x\in F_{ab}$ and $y\in F_{ba}$, or the reverse assignment.
Every such edge is an original missing pair. Assume that all edges
of $\mathcal B_Z$ are good. This is automatic when $|Z|\le3$, since
an internal bad pair of $Z$ would require four distinct vertices of $Z$.

Orient $\mathcal B_Z$ by the canonical convenient orientations of
its edges, and put
\[
 S_{\mathcal B}=\{z\in Z:d^+_{\mathcal B_Z}(z)=0\}.
\]
This graph contains only cross-witness pairs, not every missing pair
inside $Z$, and not the original arcs of $D[Z]$.

\begin{lemma}[Cross-witness source transfer]
\label{lem:cross-witness-source-transfer}
If the chosen protection-compatible reference order gives
\[
 t(z)\le g(z)+1\qquad(z\in Z\setminus S_{\mathcal B}),
\]
then $D$ has a Seymour vertex.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We check the two uses of the source graph in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:small-failure-sources}. Although $S_{\mathcal B}$ may
contain an original missing pair not belonging to $\mathcal B_Z$,
no bad pair can have failures from $S_{\mathcal B}$ in both directions.
Such opposite sources would form a cross-witness edge, and its
orientation would give one of them positive out-degree in
$\mathcal B_Z$. Thus every bad pair can still be oriented so as to
avoid failure sources in $S_{\mathcal B}$.

Complete with these bad directions and all canonical good directions,
and choose a median feed $f$. If $f\notin Z\setminus S_{\mathcal B}$,
reverse all its added outgoing arcs. The original first neighbourhood
is retained, and no added second arc produces a failure from $f$.
The feed SNP therefore transfers back to $D$.

If $f\in Z\setminus S_{\mathcal B}$, retain one added cross-witness
arc $f\to q$ and reverse its other added outgoing arcs. This retained
arc is a canonical convenient orientation of a good missing pair,
with $f,q\in Z$. For a protected far head $y\triangleleft f$, each
predecessor of $y$ is a predecessor of $f$. Hence convenience of
$f\to q$ directly implies convenience of $y\to q$ whenever $qy$
is missing. Thus $qy$ is automatically good, independent of $|Z|$.
Canonical compatibility then orients it $y\to q$. An original arc
$q\to y$ would force $q\to f$ by protection, contrary to the
missing pair $fq$. Consequently the same exact envelope holds:
\[
 N_{T'}^{++}(f)\subseteq
 \bigl(N_D^{++}(f)\setminus\{q\}\bigr)
 \cup\bigl(\mathcal F(f)\setminus\{q\}\bigr).
\]
The first neighbourhood has gained exactly $q$, and $q$ removes one
element from the disjoint second/far envelope. Thus
$d_{T'}^+(f)-d_{T'}^{++}(f)\ge g(f)+2-t(f)\ge1$,
contradicting the feed-vertex theorem. The same backward-arc reversal
argument preserves the median order throughout.
\end{proof}

In terms of the dependency digraph, the hypothesis says that every
missing pair with positive out-degree has in-degree zero: each tail
of a losing relation is good. It excludes every directed walk of two
arcs, including a two-cycle. Merely excluding simple directed paths
on three distinct vertices would not be the same condition.

\begin{proposition}[One two-unit source among at most three sources]
\label{prop:one-two-unit-source}
Let $D$ be a strongly connected all-deficient oriented graph minimal
under deletion of arbitrary nonempty arc sets. Suppose its positive
residual support has at most three vertices, at most one of which
has residual two, with every other positive residual equal to one.
Then $D$ cannot exist. In particular, residual four cannot have
distribution $(2,1,1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $Z=\{x:\eta(x)>0\}$. Zero-residual protection guarantees that every
bad-edge directional failure source belongs to $Z$. For every
residual-one vertex, Corollary~\ref{cor:eta-one-budget} gives
$t(x)\le1\le g(x)+1$.

If there is no residual-two vertex, any canonical reference order
meets the cross-witness transfer threshold. If there is such a
vertex $u$ and $t(u)\le2$, the same is true, since $g(u)\ge1$.
In either case Lemma~\ref{lem:cross-witness-source-transfer} gives
a Seymour vertex, a contradiction.

It remains that $\eta(u)=2$ and $t(u)>2$. Each cross-witness neighbour
$r$ of $u$ has $N_D^+(r)\cap U_D(u)\ne\varnothing$. The two-unit
dichotomy, Lemma~\ref{lem:eta-two-dichotomy}, therefore makes $r\to u$
a convenient direction. Since $ru$ is missing, $r,u$ are incomparable
in the protection partial order. We may choose a linear extension
placing every such $r$ before $u$ simultaneously. Indeed, adding
these precedence constraints would create a cycle only if the
original protection order already had a path from $u$ to some $r$,
which would make the missing pair comparable.

Every cross-witness edge incident to $u$ is now canonically directed
into $u$: a unique convenient direction is already inward, and a
double-convenient edge is directed inward by the chosen order.
Thus $u\in S_{\mathcal B}$. Only residual-one vertices require a
budget, and they have it. Applying the cross-witness transfer gives
the required contradiction. This proof includes $P(u)=\varnothing$;
no bound on $t(u)$ was inferred from that case.
\end{proof}


The later support theorem excludes every two-positive allocation; no such allocation remains a frontier problem.
