\section{A near-whole feed exposes its unprotected heads in an original third layer}
\label{sec:nearwhole-feed-global-boundary-budget}

Let $D$ be strongly connected, all-deficient and minimal under arbitrary
nonempty arc-set deletion. Let $Q$ be a terminal weak protection closure
and $T_*$ a maximum reference completion, as previously defined. Fix a
global median feed $f$ and suppose $\mu_D(f)=1$; write $r$ for its sole
original missing partner. All sets below, except those explicitly indexed
by $Q$ or $T_*$, are original sets in $D$.

\begin{lemma}[A sole remaining partner is an old second target]
\label{lem:weak-sole-partner-original-second}
One has $\mathcal M_Q(f)=\{r\}$ and $r\in N_D^{++}(f)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The original feed gap is positive. The closure theorem excludes
a terminal whole feed, while $\mathcal M_Q(f)\subseteq\mathcal M_D(f)$.
Thus $r$ is the sole remaining missing partner. The feed is maximal in
terminal protection, so absence of the weak $r\to f$ step supplies
$p\in N_Q^-(r)\setminus N_Q^-(f)$. The vertices $p,f,r$ are distinct.
Every pair $pf$ is already adjacent in $Q$, because $r$ is the only missing
partner; the direction cannot be $p\to f$, so $f\to p$ in $Q$.
Its first neighborhood is original, so $f\to p$ is original.
The retained path $f\to p\to r$ proves $r\in N_Q^{++}(f)$;
exact original second equality at feeds gives $r\in N_D^{++}(f)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Global original boundary charges every unprotected head]
\label{prop:nearwhole-feed-third-layer-budget}
Assume additionally $P_D(f)=\varnothing$. Put
$d=d_D^+(f)$, $g=g_D(f)$, $t=t_D(f)$ and $O=N_D^+(f)$.
Then
\[
 \{f\}\,\dot\cup\,\mathcal F_D(f)\subseteq\partial_D^2O,
 \qquad d\ge g+t+2,
 \qquad n\ge g+3t+5.
\]
In particular at $(\eta_D(f),g)=(2,1)$ one has $t\ge2$ and
$d\ge t+3$; at $(\eta_D(f),g)=(3,2)$ one has $t\ge3$ and
$d\ge t+4$. These are original-global-minimality constraints, not
conditions imposed on an altered closure graph.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The sole missing partner is an original exact second target by the lemma.
Every unprotected far head $y$ is therefore an original incoming neighbor
of $f$. Failure of protection supplies a predecessor $p\to y$ which
does not point to $f$. It is not $f$, and cannot be an original first
neighbor of $f$, since that would reach $y$ in two steps. All other
vertices except $r$ are adjacent to $f$, so this predecessor must be $r$.
Thus $r\to y$ originally for every $y\in\mathcal F_D(f)$.

Since $\partial_D O=N_D^{++}(f)$ contains $r$, and no far head is
in $O\cup N_D^{++}(f)$, these heads lie in $\partial_D^2O$.
Also choose any $a\in O$, which is nonempty. The hypothesis $P_D(f)$
empty says $a$ reaches $f$ within two steps. It cannot point directly
to $f$, because $f\to a$ is original. Hence some original
$a\to w\to f$ exists. The vertex $w$ is an original exact second
target of $f$ and points to $f$. Thus $f\in\partial_D^2O$ too.

The original external-boundary minimality lemma now gives
\[
 t+1\le|\partial_D^2O|<|\partial_D O|=d-g,
\]
so integrality yields $d\ge g+t+2$. The total original far degree is
$n-1-d-(d-g)=n-1-2d+g$, which is at least $t$.
Therefore $n\ge1+2d-g+t\ge g+3t+5$.
Finally $P_D(f)$ empty and $\mu_D(f)=1$ give
$\eta_D(f)=2+g-1=g+1$, while strict original closure surplus gives
$t\ge g+1$. The two numerical cases follow.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the previous local control is insufficient.}
The ten-vertex stable control has original first set
$O=\{1,2,3,4\}$, original boundary $\{5,6,7\}$, and external second
boundary $\{0,8,9\}$. Their sizes are equal. Hence it violates the
strict original-global boundary inequality even though all outgoing-star
deletions at its feed are locally critical. This pinpoints a concrete
global constraint missing from that local model. The present degree
bound still leaves arbitrarily large feasible parameter ranges; it is
not an exclusion of the stable low-residual branch or a full SNC proof.
