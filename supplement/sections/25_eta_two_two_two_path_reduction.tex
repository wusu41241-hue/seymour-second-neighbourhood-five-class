\section{A reduction of the residual allocation $(2,2,2)$}
\label{sec:two-two-two-path-reduction}

Assume $D$ is strongly connected, all-deficient, and minimal under
arbitrary nonempty arc-set deletion. Suppose its positive residual
support is $\{c,v,w\}$, each of residual two. Let $\mathcal B$
be the actual cross-witness graph. Its edges are original missing
pairs, and every actual directional failure source is a nonisolated
vertex of this graph.

\begin{lemma}[Retaining a good added first arc]
\label{lem:eta222-retain-good}
Complete the good pairs canonically and the bad pairs arbitrarily.
If a global median feed $f$ has a good added out-arc $f\to q$
and $t(f)\le g_D(f)+1$, then $D$ has a Seymour vertex.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $D$ is all-deficient. Retain $f\to q$ and reverse every
other added out-arc of $f$. The same order is still global median.
Its first neighborhood is $N_D^+(f)\dot\cup\{q\}$.
Protected-target compatibility excludes every path through $q$
to a protected far target. An original first intermediate followed
by a chosen bad arc can introduce only an unprotected far head;
original or convenient second arcs introduce no far head. Hence
\[
 N_{T'}^{++}(f)\subseteq
 (N_D^{++}(f)\setminus\{q\})
 \cup(\mathcal F(f)\setminus\{q\}).
\]
Because $fq$ was missing, $q$ belongs to the disjoint union on the
right before removal. Therefore
$g_{T'}(f)\ge g_D(f)+2-t(f)\ge1$, contradicting the tournament
feed theorem.
No restriction on $t$ at other vertices is used here.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two large star leaves are adjacent]
\label{lem:eta222-large-leaves-adjacent}
Suppose $\mathcal B$ is the two-edge path centered at $c$, and
$t(v),t(w)>2$. Then $vw$ is an original adjacent pair.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $P(v)$ or $P(w)$ is empty, its vertex has exactly one missing
partner, namely $c$, so $vw$ is adjacent.
Otherwise both large-unit packets have $s=0,j=1$, and their unique
exceptional missing partner is $c$. Saturation gives
\[
 \mathcal M(v)\setminus\{c\}\subseteq N_D^{++}(v),\qquad
 \mathcal M(w)\setminus\{c\}\subseteq N_D^{++}(w).
\]
Suppose $vw$ were missing. They then reach each other at exact
distance two. Thus $w\notin P(v)$ and
$w\in N_D^{--}(v)\setminus\{c\}=\partial P(v)$;
the corresponding assertion holds with $v,w$ interchanged.
A predecessor $p\in P(v)$ with $p\to w$ makes $w\to v$
nonconvenient. A predecessor in $P(w)$ similarly makes $v\to w$
nonconvenient. Hence $vw$ would be bad. A bad pair requires two
distinct actual directional failure sources outside its endpoints.
Only $c$ is available, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two adjacent vertices admit inward reference priorities]
\label{lem:eta222-two-inward-priorities}
If $vw$ is an original adjacent pair, there is a reference linear
extension of the protected-target partial order placing every
missing partner of $v$ before $v$, and every missing partner of $w$
before $w$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Adding either family of priorities alone cannot create a cycle:
an original protected chain between a vertex and its missing partner
would force their adjacency. Suppose adding both creates a cycle,
and take a shortest directed cycle in the relation generated by the
old order and the two new priority families. After contracting old
chains, it must use each new head $v,w$ once. Thus there are missing
partners $r$ of $v$ and $q$ of $w$ with old protected chains
$v\triangleleft q$ and $w\triangleleft r$. Neither chain has
length zero, since $vw$ is adjacent.
If $w\to v$, predecessor containment along the first chain forces
$w\to q$, contrary to missing $wq$. If $v\to w$, the second
chain forces $v\to r$, contrary to missing $vr$.
These are the two possible orientations of the original adjacent
pair, so the cycle is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The path case of $(2,2,2)$ is impossible]
\label{prop:eta222-no-path}
Under the displayed residual allocation, $\mathcal B$ cannot be
a two-edge path.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Label its center $c$. The large-two-unit witness-degree lemma gives
$t(c)\le2$. Orient all bad pairs so that their selected direction
fails only at $c$. Every added out-arc of either leaf is good:
failure of a selected bad out-arc at $c$ would require the original
arc $c\to v$ or $c\to w$, whereas both pairs are missing.

If no leaf has $t>2$, all three actual sources satisfy
$t\le2\le g_D+1$, and the generic source transfer applies;
all internal missing pairs among the three actual sources are good.
Otherwise choose the reference priorities of the large-leaf
completion lemma for every large leaf. With one large leaf this is
already available. With two, Lemmas
\ref{lem:eta222-large-leaves-adjacent} and
\ref{lem:eta222-two-inward-priorities} permit both families
simultaneously. The proof of the leaf-completion lemma
then keeps every large leaf deficient in this fixed tournament, and
adds $c\to v$ for a chosen large leaf $v$.

Take any global median feed $f$.
It cannot be a large leaf, whose tournament gap is positive.
If $f=c$, retain its good added arc $c\to v$ and use
Lemma~\ref{lem:eta222-retain-good}, since $t(c)\le2$.
If $f$ is a small leaf and has an added out-arc, that arc is good
and the same retention lemma applies. If it has no added out-arc,
its original first arcs acquire no new far head, since the selected
bad directions fail only at $c$; its SNP transfers directly to $D$.
Finally, if $f$ is outside the actual source set, reverse all its
added out-arcs. The original first arcs have no chosen-direction
failures, so once more its SNP transfers directly to $D$.
This last argument does not require $t(f)=0$ and avoids improperly
claiming that every outside vertex stayed deficient in the original
completed tournament. All possible feeds lead to a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A large nonselected vertex with a safe packet]
\label{lem:eta222-large-nonselected}
Suppose selected bad directions fail only at $c$, and $x\ne c$
has $\eta(x)=2$ and $t(x)>2$. Choose canonical good directions
using a reference extension placing all good missing partners of
$x$ before $x$. If either $c\notin P(x)$, or every head of every selected
bad arc is originally reachable from $x$ within two steps, then the
resulting tournament keeps $x$ deficient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $P(x)$ is empty, every missing direction into $x$ is convenient;
thus no incident pair is bad, every added incident arc is directed
into $x$, and its second neighborhood is unchanged.
Otherwise the large-two-unit packet has $s=0,j=1$ and a unique
exceptional partner $q$. Its direction $q\to x$ is convenient,
and saturation gives
\[
 E=(\mathcal M(x)\setminus\{q\})\dot\cup\partial^2P(x)
   =N_D^{++}(x).
\]
In particular $xq$ is good and is selected inward by the reference
rule. Every added first neighbor $r$ of $x$ is nonexceptional.
Moreover $r\to x$ is nonconvenient: for a good pair this follows
from the reference priority, and for a bad pair it follows from
badness itself. Consequently some $p\in P(x)$ points to $r$.

For $y\in U_D(x)$, the saturated partner budget excludes an
original $r\to y$. Every path of length at most two from $P(x)$
stays in $P(x)\cup\partial P(x)\cup\partial^2P(x)$, all of
whose vertices are originally reachable from $x$. Thus $y$ is
far from $p$. An added good direction $r\to y$ is impossible
because it fails at $p$. An added bad direction would also fail
at $p$, impossible when $c\notin P(x)$. Under the alternative
hypothesis it is impossible because $y$ is an originally far head
of $x$. Original first intermediates create no far head since
selected directions do not fail at $x$.
Writing $K=N_T^+(x)\setminus N_D^+(x)$, saturation also gives
$K\subseteq N_D^{++}(x)$. Hence
\[
 N_T^{++}(x)=N_D^{++}(x)\setminus K,
 \qquad g_T(x)=g_D(x)+2|K|>0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Adjacency of an inactive large vertex]
\label{lem:eta222-inactive-adjacency}
Suppose the actual source graph is the single edge $uv$, both
endpoints have residual two and $t>2$, and $w$ has residual two
and $t(w)>2$ but is not an actual source. Then $w$ is adjacent
to at least one of $u,v$. If $wc$ is missing for one endpoint
$c$, then $c\notin P(w)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exceptional partner of each large active endpoint is its sole
witness partner, namely the other endpoint. Thus if $wc$ is
missing, saturation at $c$ puts $w\in N_D^{++}(c)$ and gives
$c\notin P(w)$.
If $P(w)$ is empty, $w$ has only one missing partner and the
adjacency assertion is immediate. Otherwise let $q$ be its unique
exceptional partner. If $wc$ is missing and $c\ne q$, saturation
at $w$ also gives $c\in N_D^{++}(w)$. The two vertices reach
each other at exact distance two and are nonexceptional in each
other's packets. They therefore belong to each other's full
boundaries $\partial P$; the two predecessor witnesses make
both directions of $wc$ nonconvenient. This would make $wc$ bad,
but its two actual witnesses must avoid its endpoints and there is
only the other active source available. This is impossible.
Thus any missing endpoint must equal $q$, and $w$ cannot be
missing from both distinct endpoints.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exclusion of $(2,2,2)$]
\label{thm:eta222-excluded}
The positive residual allocation $(2,2,2)$ is impossible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The source graph is nonempty and is supported on three vertices.
A triangle is excluded by the witness-degree bound and generic
source transfer. A two-edge path is excluded by
Proposition~\ref{prop:eta222-no-path}. It remains that the actual
sources are the endpoints $u,v$ of one edge, with the third positive
vertex $w$ inactive. Internal missing pairs among these three
vertices are all good.

If both active endpoints have $t\le2$, use the generic source
transfer. If exactly one active endpoint is large, select the other
as the sole failure source $c$, and use the large-leaf reference
priorities to keep the large endpoint deficient. Its completion
adds a good arc out of $c$. A median feed outside the actual source
set transfers after reversing all added first arcs; the large leaf
cannot be feed; and a feed at $c$ is excluded by retaining its added
good arc, since $t(c)\le2$. Thus both endpoints must be large.

Orient all bad pairs as a single-blame completion. They are exactly
$A\times B$, where
\[
 A=N_D^+(u)\cap U_D(v),\qquad
 B=N_D^+(v)\cap U_D(u).
\]
Either endpoint can be chosen as the sole selected source. If
$t(w)\le2$, choose either endpoint $c$, use the leaf lemma
to keep the other endpoint deficient, and use the nonselected-source
budget to keep $w$ deficient. All zero-residual vertices likewise
stay deficient. This fixed sink-free tournament would have at most
one Seymour vertex, contradicting the two-vertex tournament theorem.

Now assume $t(w)>2$. If $w$ is missing from an active endpoint
$c$, choose $c$ as the selected source. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:eta222-inactive-adjacency}, the other endpoint $x$
is adjacent to $w$, and $c\notin P(w)$.
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:eta222-two-inward-priorities} to put all good
missing partners of both $x,w$ before them. The leaf
completion keeps $x$ deficient, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:eta222-large-nonselected} keeps $w$ deficient.

Finally suppose $w$ is adjacent to both active endpoints. If some
endpoint $c$ is not in $P(w)$, select it and use the same simultaneous
priorities for the other endpoint and $w$. If both endpoints belong
to $P(w)$, their original adjacency to $w$ must point as
$w\to u$ and $w\to v$. Every bad endpoint in $A\cup B$ is then
originally reachable from $w$ within two steps. Choose either endpoint
as $c$, and the alternative condition of
Lemma~\ref{lem:eta222-large-nonselected} keeps $w$ deficient;
the same simultaneous priorities keep the opposite large leaf
deficient.

In every remaining case every vertex except possibly $c$ stays
deficient in one fixed completed tournament. Zero-residual vertices
use the single-blame protected-target envelope. The
completion is sink-free because original outdegrees are positive.
The two-Seymour-vertex theorem again gives the contradiction.
\end{proof}
