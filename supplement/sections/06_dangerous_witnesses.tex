\section{Dangerous witnesses of a bad missing edge}
\label{sec:dangerous-witnesses}

For a vertex $v$, a far target $y\in U(v)$ is called protected from
$v$ if
\[
 y\to v,\qquad N^-(y)\subseteq N^-(v)\setminus\{y\}.
\]
Let $t(v)$ be the number of far targets not protected from $v$.
We first prove a completion statement for an arbitrary oriented
graph whose vertices all fail the second-neighbourhood inequality.
In this statement $g(v)=d^+(v)-d^{++}(v)$ is any positive integer.

A missing edge $ab$ is good in Ghazal's sense \cite{Ghazal2012} if at least one of
\[
 \forall x\notin\{a,b\},\quad
 x\to a\Longrightarrow b\in N^+(x)\cup N^{++}(x),
\]
\[
 \forall x\notin\{a,b\},\quad
 x\to b\Longrightarrow a\in N^+(x)\cup N^{++}(x)
\]
holds. Such an implication specifies its convenient orientation.
A failure for the orientation $a\to b$ is a source $x$ with
$x\to a$ and $b\in U(x)$.

\begin{lemma}[A localized completion obstruction]
\label{lem:dangerous-completion}
Let $D$ be any nonempty oriented graph with $g(v)>0$ for every vertex. Then
there is a bad missing edge $ab$ and distinct witnesses $u,v$ such that
\[
 u\to a,\quad b\in U(u),\qquad
 v\to b,\quad a\in U(v),
 \qquad t(u)\ge g(u),\quad t(v)\ge g(v).
\]
The witnesses $u,v$ are non-adjacent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $B_t=\{x:t(x)\ge g(x)\}$. Suppose the conclusion fails. Every bad
missing edge then has at least one orientation with no failure whose
source belongs to $B_t$. Give it such an orientation. Give every good
missing edge a \emph{fully convenient} orientation, as in Ghazal's
completion proof. For good edges we do not merely choose an orientation
avoiding failures from $B_t$.

The completed graph is a tournament $T$. Choose a median order and let
$f$ be its last, or feed, vertex. Reverse every added arc leaving $f$
so that all added arcs incident to $f$ point towards it; call the
result $T'$. The order remains a local median order: interval feedback
conditions not involving $f$ do not change, and feedback conditions
involving its last position only improve. By the feed-vertex theorem
\cite{HavetThomasse2000},
$f$ has the second-neighbourhood property in $T'$. Its first
out-neighbourhood is exactly the original $N_D^+(f)$.

Let $E=N_{T'}^{++}(f)\setminus N_D^{++}(f)$ be the set of new second
\emph{heads}. For $z\in E$, choose a two-step path $f\to a\to z$
in $T'$. The first arc is original. The second must be an added arc;
otherwise $z$ would already be an original second neighbour. Neither
$a$ nor $z$ equals $f$, since a tournament has no two-cycle. Thus the
second arc was not among those reversed at the feed vertex, and still
has its chosen completion orientation. The equality of the first
neighbourhoods ensures that $z\in U_D(f)$.

This added edge cannot be good, since its convenient orientation
allows no such failure. It is therefore a bad edge $az$ with a failure
from $f$. Any failure head of a bad edge is unprotected: an opposite
failure witness $w\to z$, $a\in U(w)$ would, if $z$ were protected
from $f$, imply $w\to f\to a$, a contradiction.

If $f\in B_t$, our choice of orientations excludes the failure, so
$E$ is empty. If $f\notin B_t$, we have
\[
 E\subseteq\{y\in U_D(f):y\text{ is unprotected from }f\},
 \qquad |E|\le t(f)<g(f).
\]
The count is of distinct heads, not arcs or two-step paths.
In either case,
\[
 |N_{T'}^{++}(f)|\le d_D^{++}(f)+|E|<d_D^+(f)
                         =|N_{T'}^+(f)|,
\]
contradicting the feed-vertex theorem. Hence some bad edge has failures
from $B_t$ in both directions.

The two witnesses are distinct and outside $\{a,b\}$. An arc $u\to v$
would yield $u\to v\to b$, and an arc $v\to u$ would yield
$v\to u\to a$. Both are forbidden. Thus $u,v$ are non-adjacent.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The safe residual budget]
\label{lem:safe-residual-unprotected}
Let $D$ be a strongly connected counterexample minimal under deletion
of any nonempty set of arcs. If $\eta(v)<g(v)$, then
\[
 {t(v)\le\eta(v)<g(v).}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The established gap and residual theorems give $g(v)\in\{1,2\}$ and
$\eta(v)\ge0$. Put $P=P(v)$, $R=N^{--}(v)$, and $W=N^-(v)$.
If $P$ is empty, the identity
$\eta(v)=2\mu(v)+g(v)-1$ and $\eta(v)<g(v)$ force $\mu(v)=0$.
Every far target $y$ then points to $v$, and every $x\to y$ must
point to $v$, since otherwise $v\to x\to y$. Thus all far targets
are protected and $t(v)=0$.

Suppose $P\ne\varnothing$, and write $C=\partial P\subseteq R$,
$j=|R\setminus C|$, and $s=|N^+(v)\cap(R\setminus C)|$.
The previously proved outgoing-bundle refinement gives
\[
 s>0\Longrightarrow\eta(v)\ge g(v)+2(j-s),
 \qquad
 s=0\Longrightarrow\eta(v)\ge2j.
\]
Since $s\le j$, the safe inequality excludes $s>0$. Also
$\eta(v)<g(v)\le2$ gives $\eta(v)\le1$, and hence $j=0$.
Thus $C=R$.

Let $X=\partial^2P$. Then $X\subseteq W$, and the external-boundary
lemma gives $|X|\le|R|-1$. Every vertex of $Y=W\setminus X$ is a
protected far target of $v$: no vertex of $P$ or $R$ points to it,
and its in-neighbours therefore lie in $W\setminus\{y\}$.
Using $\eta(v)=\mu(v)-1+|R|-d^{++}(v)$, we obtain
\[
 |Y|\ge|W|-|R|+1=|U(v)|-\eta(v).
\]
Consequently at most $\eta(v)$ far targets can be unprotected.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dangerous residual witnesses]
\label{prop:dangerous-residual-witnesses}
Every strongly connected counterexample minimal under deletion of any
nonempty set of arcs has a bad missing edge with opposite failure
witnesses $u,v$ such that $u,v$ are non-adjacent and
\[
 {\eta(u)\ge g(u),\qquad\eta(v)\ge g(v).}
\]
In particular,
\[
 2M-n=\sum_x\eta(x)\ge g(u)+g(v).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:dangerous-completion}. The contrapositive of
Lemma~\ref{lem:safe-residual-unprotected} says that
$t(x)\ge g(x)$ implies $\eta(x)\ge g(x)$. Non-negativity of all
residuals proves the sum bound.
\end{proof}

This strengthens the strict missing-pair theorem established earlier.
If every vertex has gap two, then $2M-n\ge4$, and parity yields
\[
 {M\ge\lceil n/2\rceil+2.}
\]
If instead $2M-n=2$, then the two dangerous witnesses are exactly the
two positive-residual vertices; each has residual one and gap one,
and they are non-adjacent.
