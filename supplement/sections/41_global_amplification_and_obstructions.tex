\section{A dense amplification that preserves the local obstruction}
\label{sec:global-dense-amplification}

For any oriented graph $D$ with $n$ vertices, form $\mathcal C(D)$ from
three disjoint copies $D_0,D_1,D_2$, adding every arc from $D_i$ to
$D_{i+1}$ cyclically. There are no digons. The complete cyclic connections
make $\mathcal C(D)$ strongly connected even if $D$ was not.
This is the three-copy specialization of the cycle blow-up mechanism
used by Espuny Diaz, Girao, Granet and Kronenberg~\cite{EspunyDiazEtAl2025} in their counterexample
reductions; the exact invariants below are recorded explicitly.

\begin{lemma}[Exact invariants of cyclic amplification]
\label{lem:global-cyclic-amplification}
For a copy $x_i$ of $x\in D$, all first, exact second, and far relations
satisfy
\[
\begin{split}
 N_{\mathcal C(D)}^+(x_i)&=N_D^+(x)_i\,\dot\cup\,V(D_{i+1}),\\
 N_{\mathcal C(D)}^{++}(x_i)&=N_D^{++}(x)_i\,\dot\cup\,V(D_{i+2}),\\
 P_{\mathcal C(D)}(x_i)&=P_D(x)_i,\qquad
 \mathcal F_{\mathcal C(D)}(x_i)=\mathcal F_D(x)_i.
\end{split}
\]
Thus $g,\mu,\eta$, and the actual bad failure-source status are preserved
pointwise. The global quantities obey
\[
 n'=3n,\quad\delta'=n+\delta,\quad M'=3M,\quad R'=3R,
 \qquad n'-2\delta'=n-2\delta.
\]
In particular a counterexample is amplified to a strongly connected
counterexample without decreasing any of its original vertex gaps.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An internal first step introduces only internal original second targets
or vertices of the next copy, already first targets. A first step into
the next copy reaches the entire following copy. It cannot return to
the original copy within two steps. These facts prove the first two
identities. The reverse exact-second degree likewise increases by $n$.
All vertices outside the starting copy are reached in one or two steps,
so far and far-in relations stay internal. Every internal incoming
neighborhood gets the same previous-copy set, preserving protection.

All missing pairs stay inside copies. An outside predecessor of one
endpoint of a missing pair already reaches its other endpoint in one
or two steps; it is not a new directional failure witness. Therefore
the old failure sets and actual source status are copied exactly.
The stated degree, residual, and missing-pair formulas follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An asymptotically dense global target]
\label{cor:global-dense-target}
If SNC were proved for every oriented graph with
$\delta^+(D)>(1/2-\varepsilon)|V(D)|$, for one fixed $\varepsilon>0$,
then SNC would hold for all finite oriented graphs.
More generally, it suffices to prove that for every fixed defect
$q=n-2\delta$, counterexamples of that defect have bounded order.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Iterating the lemma gives $n_k=3^kn$ and
$\delta_k=(n_k-q)/2$, with the same fixed $q$.
The ratio $\delta_k/n_k=1/2-q/(2n_k)$ tends to one half from below,
and any initial counterexample persists at unbounded order. It would
eventually violate either proposed theorem, yielding the contradiction.
\end{proof}

This is a genuine global reduction but is not an easier theorem already
proved here. Crucially, it does not preserve strong arc-set minimality:
if $D$ is all-deficient, deleting \emph{all} cross-copy arcs leaves three
all-deficient copies. Minimizing arcs can therefore discard the high
minimum degree. A bound proved only for arc-minimal graphs cannot simply
be applied to the dense amplifications. Likewise the pointwise residual
is unchanged; the amplification is not a reduction to pure-unit residuals.

\section{The exact weighted transfer problem}
\label{sec:global-weighted-transfer}

Let $N_D$ have entry $+1$ for an exact second target, $-1$ for a first
target, and zero otherwise. Seacrest's Farkas alternative~\cite{Seacrest2015} relates an
expansion weighting to a weighted counterexample in the reverse graph.
It is equivalent to SNC in general, not a proof of either alternative.
For tournaments, Fisher's losing-density result supplies a nonzero
$\lambda\ge0$ with $N_T^T\lambda\ge0$.

In any protection-preserving completion define $A_x$ to be the newly
reached originally far second heads. Original second paths persist
unless their endpoints have become first neighbors. Hence the exact
gap difference is
\[
 g_T(x)-g_D(x)=2|K_x|-c_x,
 \qquad c_x=|K_x\cap\mathcal F_D(x)|+|A_x|\le t(x).
\]
The two cost sets are disjoint. If a tournament losing density could be
chosen with
\[
 \sum_x\lambda_x(2|K_x|-c_x)\ge0,
\]
then all-deficiency of $D$ would contradict
\[
 0\ge\sum_x\lambda_x g_T(x)
   =\sum_x\lambda_x g_D(x)+
      \sum_x\lambda_x(2|K_x|-c_x)>0.
\]
This is a global mass-transport target, allowing different vertices to
compensate one another. Existence of a suitable completion and losing
density has not been proved. The looser replacement $c_x\le t(x)$ can
discard exactly the slack needed even in an ordinary directed four-cycle.

For an arbitrary incomplete oriented graph, an equilibrium of its skew
adjacency matrix alone does not suffice: the positive five-vertex control
weights in the companion file balance incoming and outgoing weights but
fail second-neighborhood expansion. This is not a tournament control:
Fisher's tournament losing densities do satisfy reverse expansion, and
the follow-up density-collapse lemma proves equality of the two normalized
feasible sets. The completion, rather than an extra density degree of
freedom, is the remaining choice in that formulation.
Nor does the existential arc-weighted tournament theorem authorize using
an arbitrary arc-weighted median feed: the canonical six-vertex control
has a verified such feed with arc-first weight $74$ and arc-second weight
$38$. These are failures of proposed proof shortcuts, not conjecture
counterexamples.

\section{The missing global step}
\label{sec:global-open-step}

The two promising concrete tasks are now:
(i) a quantitative extension of maximal reference completion that handles
a nonunit actual feed and its multiple inverse witnesses; or
(ii) a completion/losing-density selection proving the exact weighted
mass balance above. Simultaneous packet control is a third sufficient
route, but it must establish independence and acyclic reference priorities,
not assume them separately for every target.

No terminating reduction from arbitrary mixed residuals to the excluded
pure-unit class has been obtained. The preceding amplified graphs show
why density growth, vertex-weight reformulation, or arc minimization alone
does not provide that reduction.
