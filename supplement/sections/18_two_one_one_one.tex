\section{Excluding the residual allocation (2,1,1,1)}
\label{sec:exclude-two-one-one-one}

Continue in a finite nonempty strongly connected all-deficient graph
minimal under deletion of arbitrary nonempty arc sets.
Suppose its positive residual vertices are a heavy vertex $u$ with
$\eta(u)=2$ and three unit vertices with residual one.
Every other vertex has residual zero and hence $t=0$; every unit
vertex has $t\le1$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:eta-one-budget}.
Let $\mathcal B$ be the actual cross-witness graph on these four
vertices. It is nonempty, and its edges are original missing pairs.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:no-two-one-one-one}
The positive residual allocation $(2,1,1,1)$ is impossible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{No unit--unit witness edge.}\quad 
If no edge of $\mathcal B$ joins two units, it is a star centered at
$u$. Orient each bad pair so that its only selected failure source
is $u$, and good pairs canonically. All other vertices remain
strictly deficient in the same completed tournament by
Lemma~\ref{lem:single-selected-source-completion}.
There is at most one Seymour vertex. The tournament has no sink,
contradicting Havet--Thomasse's two-Seymour-vertex theorem
\cite[Theorem~2]{HavetThomasse2000}.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Classification after a unit--unit edge.}\quad 
Let $ab$ be a unit--unit edge of $\mathcal B$, and let $c$ be the
third unit. Lemma~\ref{lem:unit-unit-witness-tail-bad} shows that
the original missing pair $ab$ is itself bad.
Its opposite witnesses must be the two remaining positive vertices
$u,c$. After exchanging $a,b$ if necessary, their original relations
are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2111-original-configuration}
 u\to a,\qquad c\to b,\qquad
 a\in U_D(c),\qquad b\in U_D(u),
 \qquad uc\in E(\mathcal B).
\end{equation}
The unique unprotected far target of $c$ is thus $h(c)=a$.
The original arcs prohibit witness edges $ua$ and $cb$.
There is no witness edge $ca$ either: a cross witness opposite $c$
would have to point to its unique unprotected head $a$, impossible
when that witness is $a$ itself.
Consequently $\mathcal B$ is either the matching
$\{ab,uc\}$ or the path $a-b-u-c$.
In particular this classification leaves no additional unit--unit
edge or alternative four-source graph.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The path.}\quad 
If $ub\in E(\mathcal B)$, the heavy vertex $u$ has witness degree
two, so Lemma~\ref{lem:eta-two-witness-degree} gives $t(u)\le2$.
Use the bipartition $L=\{b,c\}$, $R=\{a,u\}$ and complete according
to Lemma~\ref{lem:bipartite-selected-source-completion}.
Zero-residual vertices remain strictly deficient.
The nonselected unit $a$ remains strictly deficient: with no new first
neighbour its gap is unchanged, and otherwise its gap is at least
$g_D(a)+2-t(a)>0$.
The nonselected heavy $u$ likewise remains strictly deficient,
using $t(u)\le2$.
The selected units $b,c$ have gap at least zero, and strictly positive
gap if they gain a first neighbour.
Thus all tournament gaps are nonnegative, and its zero-gap set has
at most two vertices. The tournament has no sink. This contradicts
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonnegative-tournament-three-zeros}.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The matching and its two families of bad pairs.}\quad 
Suppose now $\mathcal B=\{ab,uc\}$. Each bad pair has singleton
opposite failure sets corresponding to one of these two edges;
otherwise the complete cross-witness graph between its two failure
sets would create another edge in $\mathcal B$.
Since $h(c)=a$, the bad pairs witnessed by $u,c$ are all of the form
$ax$, with selected direction $x\to a$ failing at $c$.
Put $r=h(b)$ and $y=h(a)$. The units $a,b$ witness precisely the
single bad pair $ry$, with original arcs $a\to r$, $b\to y$,
$y\in U_D(a)$ and $r\in U_D(b)$. Select $r\to y$, which fails at $a$.
These two families are disjoint: the second pair avoids both $a,b$,
whereas every pair in the first family contains $a$.
This is the completion with bipartition
$L=\{a,c\}$, $R=\{b,u\}$.

Observe that $r\ne u$: otherwise $a\to r=u$ would contradict the
original arc $u\to a$ in
\eqref{eq:2111-original-configuration}.
Every selected bad arc incident to $u$ therefore points into $u$.
The star family cannot contain $u$ as an endpoint, since its witnesses
include $u$; the sole other pair $ry$ can contain $u$ only as $y$,
and is then directed $r\to u$.

Choose the reference extension for good directions so that all good
missing partners of $u$ precede $u$.
If $P(u)=\varnothing$, every direction into $u$ is convenient,
so no first neighbour is added there. Its original first intermediates
have no selected-direction failures, and $u$ stays deficient.
Otherwise let the target-bundle sets be
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P=P(u),\quad C=\partial P,\quad X=\partial^2P,\quad H=N_D^{--}(u)\setminus C,\\
 B=H\cap\mathcal M(u),\quad k=|B|,\quad s=|H\cap N_D^+(u)|.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Use the envelope $E$ and remainder $L_0$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:partner-head-budget}. Its size satisfies
\[
 |L_0|\le\lambda=
 \begin{cases}2-2k,&s=0,\\2-g_D(u)-2k,&s>0.\end{cases}
\]
Write $K=N_T^+(u)\setminus N_D^+(u)$.
Every member of $K$ comes from a good missing pair. By the reference
choice its reverse direction is nonconvenient, so it has an original
predecessor $p\in P$ pointing to it.
Every exceptional partner in $B$ is conveniently directed into $u$,
and every bad incident direction is already incoming. Thus
$K\subseteq\mathcal M(u)\setminus B\subseteq E$.

We will rule out a new far head outside $L_0$ through each added
first intermediate. An original second arc is excluded by the
partner-head budget. A good second arc is also excluded: a far target
outside $L_0$ is outside $P\cup C\cup X$, so it is far from the
chosen predecessor $p\in P$, making that outgoing direction
nonconvenient. This is the same cone argument as in the shared-envelope
proof, and does not suppose that $p$ has residual zero.
For a bad second arc, the star family has head $a$, already an
original first neighbour of $u$. It cannot be a far second head.
The only other possibility is $r\to y$ through the added keeper $r$.
If $y=u$, it is a self endpoint and is not a second neighbour.
Assume $y\ne u$ and that this is a genuinely new far head outside
$L_0$. Its predecessor $p\in P$ is far from $y$, so $p$ is a
directional failure witness for $r\to y$. That direction's failure
set is exactly $\{a\}$, forcing $p=a$.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{A short protected-reachability obstruction.}\quad 
The preceding putative bad path gives $a\in P(u)$, so $u\in U_D(a)$.
Since $t(a)=1$, its only unprotected head is $y$, and $y\ne u$,
the far target $u$ must be protected for $a$:
\[
 u\to a,\qquad N_D^-(u)\subseteq N_D^-(a)\setminus\{u\}.
\]
The missing pair $cu$ and $h(c)=a\ne u$ imply that $c$ reaches $u$
within two steps: otherwise $u$ would be another unprotected far
target of the unit $c$. Reaching the protected predecessor $u$
within two steps implies reaching $a$ within two steps. This
contradicts $a\in U_D(c)$ in
\eqref{eq:2111-original-configuration}.
This excludes the sole bad second arc which could create a far head
outside the packet remainder.

We have now shown
\[
 N_T^{++}(u)\subseteq
 (N_D^{++}(u)\cup L_0)\setminus K,
 \qquad K\subseteq N_D^{++}(u)\cup L_0.
\]
No disjointness of $L_0$ and the original second neighbourhood is
needed. Removing $K$ from the union gives
$g_T(u)\ge g_D(u)+2|K|-\lambda$.
If $K\ne\varnothing$, this is positive because $\lambda\le2$.
If $K=\varnothing$, its first neighbourhood is entirely original,
and no selected bad direction fails at the nonselected vertex $u$;
its gap is unchanged and positive.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The matching contradiction in the same tournament.}\quad 
The heavy $u$, the nonselected unit $b$, and all zero-residual
vertices are strictly deficient in $T$.
The selected units $a,c$ have nonnegative gaps by their one-unit
budgets. Thus every tournament gap is nonnegative, and the zero-gap
set is contained in $\{a,c\}$.
The no-sink tournament has at most two zero-gap vertices, contrary
to Lemma~\ref{lem:nonnegative-tournament-three-zeros}.
The three cases prove the theorem.
\end{proof}
