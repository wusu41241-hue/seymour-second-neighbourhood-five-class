Issues found and corrected by the main author
=============================================
1. Conflicting P_D(v) notation across chapters.
   notation.tex uses P_D(v) for the reverse-far set, while the originally
   supplied core.tex used it for N_D^-(v) intersect C. The core pattern is
   now denoted Pi_C(v), leaving reverse-far notation unchanged. This removes
   a genuine ambiguity in the hypotheses used by the residual chapter.

2. Overstatement of the partner-path theorem.
   The first version said that "any unpaid subset" not reached from the
   seeds contains an original cycle. The source theorem only asserts this
   for the entire set U=R_infinity minus Reach_{D[R_f]}(R_2). It does not
   apply to arbitrary subsets of U. The revised text explicitly defines
   this full U and correctly states that if U is nonempty, D[U] contains
   a cycle. I verified the revised wording in the saved file.

3. Positive Good-slack terminology.
   The statement that the s_L>0 branch is "stable under equality
   sedimentation" could suggest an unproved invariance of slack across
   median orders. The revised text says the cited equality move is
   unavailable and expressly disclaims equal slack in all medians.

Closure construction and rigidity
==================================
A. Simultaneous forced rounds: PASS.
   A forced a->b has a witness a triangleleft w and w->b in the preceding
   graph. Each predecessor of a points to w, proving convenience. The
   reverse direction fails at w because a is a protected far head of w.
   Thus opposite forced directions are inconsistent. For an old y
   triangleleft x, a new predecessor a->y with witness w already has
   w->x. A missing ax is forced a->x in the same batch; an old x->a
   would create a digon at w. Hence a whole forced batch preserves old
   incoming nesting. The proof does not accidentally apply sequential
   reasoning to independently chosen simultaneous weak additions.

B. Sequential weak steps: PASS.
   Only N^-(b) changes when missing a->b is added. Relations with b as
   the protected tail are excluded by the assumption that b has no
   protection successor; relations ending at b become easier. Old
   incoming containment gives the new a triangleleft b. All predicates
   are evaluated before this one step and recomputed afterwards.

C. Terminal partial domination and all-reference optimization: PASS.
   At the terminal Q, incoming nesting for y triangleleft x also gives
   outgoing nesting: x->z and missing yz would still force y->z. This
   justifies the two-position median swap even for the partial graph Q.
   Completing ALL remaining pairs forward in ANY protection extension
   preserves the same protections. The family includes all extensions,
   which is necessary for the rereferencing maximum-score argument.
   Every median of a maximizing completion has every free arc forward;
   this is a statement about all its global medians, not one chosen order.

D. Original first and second neighborhoods at the feed: PASS.
   A free outgoing arc at the feed would be backward. A closure-added
   outgoing arc would make the feed the tail of a persistent protection,
   contradicting the fact that a protection successor must occur later.
   Thus the feed's first neighborhood is unchanged at every stage.
   A new two-step path can only use an old first arc and a newly added
   second arc. Convenience in that arc's preceding graph makes its head
   already reachable within two steps. The unchanging first set prevents
   promotion or demotion between first and second classes. This reasoning
   also works for a simultaneous forced batch because the feed has no
   new first arc in the batch. Original second paths persist.
   Therefore N_T^+(f)=N_Q^+(f)=N_D^+(f) and N_Q^{++}(f)=N_D^{++}(f)
   have the scopes stated in the theorem; no equality of N_T^{++} with
   the original second set is asserted.

E. Median-score identity and feed characterization: PASS.
   For any order, the Q contribution is at most med(Q) and the r free
   pairs contribute at most r. Every Q median extends protection and
   produces a completion attaining med(Q)+r. Equality in the separate
   upper bounds characterizes the median feeds across maximizing
   completions. This does not identify their feeds with every losing-
   density support vertex or with all original actual failure sources.

Strict surplus, cycles, and equality branches
=============================================
F. Strict surplus and the zero-gap partner budget: PASS.
   A whole feed in Q would have all its Q-far heads protected and could
   acquire no second target in the completion. Together with the feed
   invariance this would leave a positive tournament gap, impossible.
   Thus a positive original-gap feed has at least one terminal missing
   partner. At zero completed gap the strong feed inequality is equality
   and G_L equals the entire completed second neighborhood. A remaining
   partner in that set would have its free incoming arc reversed relative
   to the sedimented median, contradicting free-arc rigidity. Partners
   are therefore original unprotected far vertices not paid as new heads.
   This proves t_D(f)>=g_D(f)+m. A strictly negative completed gap instead
   gives |A_f|>=g_D(f)+1 by integrality. The two cases together prove
   strict surplus without assuming all-deficiency elsewhere.

G. Original partner-cycle lifting: PASS.
   The absence of a weak r->f step gives a current predecessor p of r
   not pointing to f. Far-ness of r in T forces p->f in T; hence pf is
   missing in Q and p is another terminal partner. This gives positive
   minimum indegree in Q[R_f].
   For a forced internal addition a->b, far-ness of b and terminality
   force its witness w to lie in the same fixed final partner set R_f.
   Each new arc is thus a shortcut of a preceding internal path a->w->b.
   A simultaneous family of such shortcuts preserves acyclicity.
   A weak internal addition creating a first cycle would close an old
   path b leadsto p->a; old predecessor containment also gives p->b,
   so an old cycle would already exist. Thus closure cannot turn an
   acyclic D[R_f] into a cyclic Q[R_f]. This proves existence of an
   original cycle without asserting an edge-by-edge lift of every cycle.

H. Degree localization and low-far sufficient conditions: PASS.
   The partition identity is t-g=n-1-2d^+-h, with h the number of
   protected original far heads. Strict surplus gives 2d^++h<=n-2.
   At n=2 delta^++3, integrality forces d_D^+(f)=delta^+ and h<=1.
   The result only locates a feed whose original gap is positive; it
   does not prove it is already Seymour in the original graph.

I. Strong Good equality with negative completed gap: PASS.
   Every newly reached original far head belongs to G_L because the
   second arc of its feed path must be free and forward. Hence
   s_L=|A_f|-g_D(f)-b_L. Under s_L=0, equality sedimentation excludes
   remaining partners from G_L. Their completed-second part consists
   of old nongood second vertices and has size at most |A_f|-g_D(f);
   their far part has size at most t_D(f)-|A_f|. Thus |R_f|<=t_D(f)-g_D(f).
   The source theorem proves seed-reachability preservation and the
   original cycle on the ENTIRE unreachable set U. Its low-residual
   consequences used in extremal.tex retain strong Good equality and
   correctly permit an exceptional residual-three far partner.

Residual synthesis
==================
J. Original packet use: PASS.
   The packet is computed in the original MIN graph, not in Q or T.
   Its nonempty-P prerequisite is retained. The k=0 argument that every
   unprotected far head belongs to L handles missing heads directly and
   incoming heads through a missing predecessor. The latter cannot be
   an outneighbor of the source without creating a forbidden two-step
   path. No exclusion of exceptional heads from the external second
   boundary is used. The separately stated p=0 identity is essential.

K. Residual-four lower bound and equality: PASS.
   The zero-gap partner-cycle theorem gives m>=3, terminal partners in
   the original missing-far set S_f, and t>=g+m. For p=0, eta>=2m+g-1;
   for p>0,k>0, eta>=m+k+g*1_{s>0}; for p>0,k=0,
   eta>=g+m+g*1_{s>0}. Every branch gives eta>=m+1>=4.
   At eta=4, p=0 is excluded; m=3, s=0 and k<=1 follow. If k=1,
   the three original missing-far heads are exactly the terminal partners.
   Their directed cycle is an original triangle. Its unique exceptional
   vertex must receive an original arc from a nonexceptional partner,
   placing it in the shared remainder L in addition to the other two
   partners. This contradicts |L|<=2. Thus k=0 and g=1,t=4 follow.
   The packet partition now has J empty, so N^{--}=partial P; the
   residual identity gives |P|=2 mu-4; saturation gives L=F and the
   stated boundary equality. These are necessary shapes, not existing
   counterexample constructions.

L. The actual-source theorem: PASS as a synthesis.
   The general theorem is quoted with its actual-source and flexible
   cross-witness assumptions. The MIN corollary uses the original
   unit-residual budget and the unit--unit witness-pair property.
   It requires at least two ACTUAL sources of residual at least two;
   it does not require every positive-residual vertex to be actual.

M. R>=7 partition chain: PASS.
   At least three positive residuals and at least two residuals >=2
   leave, for R<=6, exactly (2,2,1), (3,2,1), (2,2,2), (2,2,1,1).
   The supplementary (m,2,1) theorem explicitly covers every positive
   integer m, including 2 and 3; its MIN hypotheses match the main text.
   The other two cited exclusions have the same MIN hypotheses.
   Thus the chain exhausts all possibilities and yields R>=7. Since
   R=2M-n, even n requires R>=8. The five residual-seven partitions
   printed in the main text are the exact survivors of these filters.
   Their largest residual is three, so the eta>=4 zero-gap theorem
   forces every such closure feed to have strictly negative completed
   gap; this does not exclude those partitions.

N. Sink inheritance and parity rounding: PASS.
   A sink component inherits all-deficiency and arc-set minimality:
   deleting internal arcs changes no outside first set and cannot grow
   an outside second set. The separate outside count uses only g_D<=2
   and sum_v g_H(v)>=-2M(H), so it also applies outside the strongly
   connected setting. An all-deficient graph has minimum outdegree >=2,
   hence a sink component has order k>=5.
   Let epsilon(q)=1 for even q and 0 otherwise. The required rounding is
   ceil(k/2)+epsilon(k)+ceil(3o/2)
      >=ceil((k+o)/2)+o+epsilon(k+o).
   The difference is respectively 0,1,0,0 for
   (k even,o even), (k even,o odd), (k odd,o even), (k odd,o odd).
   Therefore the additional o penalty and the equality-implies-strong
   conclusion follow. No bound is transferred backwards to a denser
   nonminimal original graph after arc deletion.

O. Near-whole feed: PASS.
   Its sole original missing partner remains missing in Q by whole-feed
   exclusion. A weak-step obstruction gives a Q path f->p->r, and feed
   second-set invariance puts r in the ORIGINAL second neighborhood.
   With reverse-far P_D(f) empty, every unprotected far head receives
   an original arc from r. Thus these heads lie in partial_D^2 O.
   A vertex a in O reaches f in exactly two original steps; the final
   intermediate cannot belong to O, which would create a digon at f.
   Hence f itself also lies in partial_D^2 O. The nonempty original
   boundary inequality gives d>=g+t+2, and the total far-vertex count
   then gives n>=g+3t+5. All minimality applications remain in D.

Additional exact arithmetic checks
==================================
An independent in-memory integer program enumerated all positive partitions
up to residual seven with the stated necessary support/heavy constraints.
It returned exactly the four low-residual partitions and five residual-seven
partitions printed in the main text. It also checked the complete sink
rounding inequality for 5<=k<=100 and 0<=o<=100: all 9,696 pairs passed.
The four symbolic parity cases above establish the general arithmetic claim;
the finite check is supplementary and not the proof.

Residual limitations preserved by the manuscript
===============================================
- A closure feed need not be an actual bad-direction source.
- Original partner cycles at zero completed gap cannot be imported without
  modification into negative completed-gap cases.
- Exceptional far heads may lie in the external second boundary and in L.
- The eta>=4 threshold concerns zero completed gap only.
- Neither strict negative completed gaps nor positive Good slack are ruled out.
- All R and M bounds concern the specified minimal-counterexample classes.

No main text was modified by this reviewer. The author applied the three
reported corrections, and the saved revised core/closure wording was checked.
